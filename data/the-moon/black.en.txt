THE MOON ― Reversed

Mist gathers along the riverbank, wrapping the reeds and stones in a silence deeper than sleep. On the muddy path, pawprints blur, indistinguishable now—one animal no different from another, their names lost to the night. The sky above is a dome without stars, a vault that gives nothing back, not even the faintest shimmer. Across the water, the old bridge becomes a memory, its timbers swallowed by thick air and the hush of unseen currents. In this hour, the common lantern sputters and fades, its feeble flame hollowed out by the weight of what cannot be seen.

In the ancient world, there were stories of seeds that sleep through the longest winters. Charred olive pits, buried beneath the hearth's ashes, waited for a spring that might never come. In high places, volcanic soil holds the remnants of cities that vanished beneath clouds of soot—columns toppled, streets erased, only fragments left to be uncovered by patient hands. At the mouth of the well, a cover forged from iron hides the depths from careless eyes; the rope disappears as it drops, and the bucket returns cold, bearing water that tastes of stone.

Under cover of another moonless night, fishermen cast their nets into water where the surface is a single, undivided sheet—no hint of ripple nor reflection. Along shorelines, cormorants dry their wings, statuesque on piers glossed with tar drawn up from the beds of ancient forests. In the house, a piece of silk lies folded at the bottom of a cedar chest, its surface taking the shape of whatever presses upon it. Shoemakers work at their bench by candle stub, brushing polish across leather until it shines with the memory of rain on slate.

Clouds gather above farmlands as storms approach, sealing off the horizon. The sound of distant bells is muffled, traveling through weather so dense that only the nearest things remain certain. Monks in mountain monasteries ink their verses onto paper made from mulberry bark, the script vanishing into the grain when the lamp gutters. In the kitchen, a cook grinds peppercorns with a granite mortar, the fragments releasing a scent that is sharp, earthy, and persistent. On the stove, a pot of beans simmers, their coats intact, holding within the promise of nourishment through lean times.

At the crossroads where all directions seem the same, travelers pause, tracing the outline of a raven feather fallen among the brambles. The feather is a sign left by someone who passed through before them, or perhaps by the wind itself. In the far north, the tundra's soil holds the memory of mammoth bones and the roots of dwarf pines, all sunk below the reach of daylight for half the year. At the edge of the village, the smith douses his iron in oil, steam rising in quick, silent clouds as the metal cools.

Tonight, one may sit beside the window and write a secret on a slip of paper, then fold it into an envelope and tuck it away. Another may grind coffee, letting its scent mark the passing of the hours before dawn. A person could rub a shoe with a scrap of old cloth, working until forgotten scuffs are erased. Or simply walk along an unlit road, letting the sounds of the night guide each step.
