THE MOON ― Reversed

A moonless night weighs quietly on the marsh, swallowing contours that once divided the hill from the lake, the wild dog from the domesticated companion. On such a night, the path between the reeds is uncertain, and the boundary between watcher and watched dissolves in the thickening mist. The stepping stones vanish under a skin of damp, and the voice of the loon calling across the water blurs into the cries of distant wolves. Behind the shiver of reeds, the world is shrouded in the same hush that once haunted the stone quarries at dusk, where workers dropped their tools and listened for the low howl of wind in the cut faces.

In these hours, a roof of slate tiles shelters those within from the sift of cold dew, the same tiles quarried from ancient hills where lines of lichen crawl over the old scars. Rain presses against these shingles, steady and persistent, its pattern echoing the rhythm of lost footsteps on cobblestone streets after midnight. In the distance, the silhouette of a chimney rises against the unyielding sky, capped with a pigeon or two, their feathers dulled by the soot and time, watching with inscrutable eyes.

Beyond the shelter, the road stretches out, winding between stone walls built by hands long gone. Each slab is heavy with the memory of foggy mornings, when shepherds led their flocks through fields bordered by moss and the silence of mist. In old towns by the sea, fishermen once patched their nets by lantern-light, their boats hauled up on beaches strewn with pebbles dark with brine. The sea itself, under these skies, becomes a mirror for secrets unspoken, waves folding quietly over stranded driftwood and the broken shells of forgotten things.

Inside, a pot simmers on the stove, its lid rattling gently. The cook reaches for a mortar carved from the same rock as the distant rooftops, grinding dried rosemary and juniper into a fine dust. The hearth is built of stone—cool to the touch, pitted with the marks of a hundred winters, holding the warmth of smoldering logs. On the table, a woolen cloak lies folded, spun from the fleeces of sheep brought in out of the rain, its weave dense enough to turn away the night air.

The air outside is thick with the promise of coming weather. Clouds pile overhead, obscuring the stars, and gutters along the eaves catch the first trickles of drizzle. Boots left by the door dry stiffly, their soles marked with the residue of walkways washed clean by persistent, whispering drizzle. It is a night for shutters latched and lanterns trimmed, for listening to the riddle of rain and the secret speech of stones.

Today, pause under the eaves and listen to the rain on the tiles, or grind spices in a stone bowl to flavor your supper. Run your fingers over the old slate on a neighbor’s roof or wrap yourself in a cloak that has weathered many seasons. Carry a pebble in your pocket, one smoothed by river or tide, and remember the hush of places worn by wind and water.
