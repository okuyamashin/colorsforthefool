THE MAGICIAN ― Upright

Upon a weathered table in a garden, the magician arranges four tools: a wand carved from hawthorn, a cup filled with clear water, a sword forged and balanced for precise work, and a coin bearing an ancient emblem. The tabletop is battered, marked by the long evidence of practice and intention. Overhead, a sign of eternity hovers, silent but certain, while below, the earth is scored with roots and small stones. The magician’s hands reach, gather, and direct; every gesture is a link between unseen possibility and the world that takes shape at the table’s edge.

In stories handed down, the pomegranate waits at the center of choices. Persephone, in the chill of the underworld, claimed a handful of seeds and so became the architect of seasons, her act dividing winter from spring. The fruit, heavy and round, sits on the magician’s table beside the cup, its tough rind giving way to countless seeds within. Each one is a promise of return—a reminder that what is hidden may be revealed with will and patience.

Across old roads and bustling markets, intent is announced with objects. In the crowded lanes of Xi’an, clusters of dried chilies dangle outside kitchens and shopfronts, catching the attention of passersby. Their presence is a signal: here is heat, here is the promise of action. Far from cities, behind the doors of modest houses, lacquered cabinets keep sachets of pepper and packets of saffron safe from damp and time. One twist of a grinder, one measured pinch, and a humble meal bursts into something memorable, the whole house sharpened by a single decision.

The record of history is made of these objects—coins pressed at the mint, sealing wax dropped onto letters, cloth dyed with the labor of many hands. The magician’s coin has its own story: traded for apples at a city market, it passes from hand to hand, a token of trust and commerce. Apples stacked on a stall call out by their brightness, their firm skin and crisp flesh an invitation to choose. In ancient tales, an apple is a gift, a challenge, a beginning.

Sometimes, weather carries a sign. In the thick of summer, a brief rain leaves the garden slick, droplets rising as mist from paving stones. Poppies, battered by the shower, let fall their petals onto the table, soft as whispers yet persistent. In the hush that follows, a rooster’s feather tumbles down, barely noticed, coming to rest near the roots beneath the magician’s chair. These are the small, ordinary traces left by the air in motion, the world in flux.

Today, you might split open a pomegranate and savor its seeds, making a quiet vow with each one. You could knot a silk ribbon round your wrist, thinking of the travelers and traders who carried bold stories and rare spices. You might slice an apple and share it, a gesture of connection and curiosity. You could hang a string of chilies by the door, to remind yourself that boldness often starts with a single, deliberate act.
