THE MAGICIAN ― Upright

A table stands beneath open sky, its surface set with four objects: a slender rod, a cup, a blade, and a weighted disk. Above, an arch of garden vines weaves a living roof, heavy with curled peas and morning dew. Below, the earth gives way to soft shoots and curling leaves, the ground’s patient work finding shape in the world above. The Magician’s hand stretches between them, fingers tracing the air as if to draw energy from sky to soil and from seed to blossom.

In ancient tales, the rod became a staff of hazel, cut from the edge of a riverbank where frogs hid under broad leaves and dragonflies hovered. The cup was carved from a gourd, dried in the shade of old willows, its bowl steeped in the scent of cut nettles and mint. The blade once belonged to a craftsman whose cottage sat atop a slope where moss grew thick on the stones and rain painted the air cool and damp. The coin, round and heavy, was pressed from the first harvest, its impression still sharp with the memory of ripening wheat and the clasp of a farmer’s hand.

The Magician’s power settles in places where earth breathes quietly: a hillside after spring rain, when the air is thick with the scent of clover and the wind moves softly through wheat fields just begun; a forest clearing, ringed by wild sorrel, where ferns uncurl and the hush is broken only by the call of a distant thrush. Here, above joins below, as the seeds beneath the soil push toward the sun, and the branches arch downward, offering new fruit. In myth, a path lined with juniper and holly leads to the place where dreams may root and grow, each step cushioned by moss and the fallen needles of ancient trees.

A city garden, shut behind a high wall, holds its own kind of magic. At dawn, the dew gathers on fresh lettuce and sage, small snails tracing glistening paths along the edges of kale. The Magician, in workman’s sleeves, might dig hands into loam, coaxing a vine along a trellis or setting a small pot of cress near a kitchen window. With each gesture, the promise of transformation lingers in the air: a sprig in a teapot, a handful of peas shelled at the table, the careful folding of grape leaves around seasoned rice.

Wind from the south brings the smell of cut grass and hay, and with it, the sense that new beginnings can be shaped from what lies at hand—a sprouting onion in the pantry, a handful of parsley in a soup, a broom woven from reeds. In the home, small acts echo the Magician’s tools: a mortar grinding fresh basil, a penny pressed into a child’s palm for luck, a walking stick whittled smooth for the next long journey. Each becomes a conduit, a way for will to shape the world, for actions to take root and bear fruit.

Today, a person could find a quiet patch of lawn or wild place and gather sprigs of mint for tea, plant a few beans in a pot, or prepare a meal that begins with a handful of fresh herbs. Tending a houseplant, arranging ferns by a window, or cleaning the air with a vase of parsley will extend the Magician’s pattern into daily life, drawing transformation from the ordinary.
