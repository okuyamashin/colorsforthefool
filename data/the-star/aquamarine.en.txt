THE STAR ― Upright

At the edge of a quiet pool, a woman kneels, pouring water from two pitchers—one stream returning to the pond, the other soaking into the earth. Around her, reeds stir and a hush settles, as if the land itself is listening. Above, a great star shines, surrounded by a gentle cluster of others, casting a soft, watchful light over the scene. It is a moment suspended between sky and water, an act of hope given form: nurturing what grows, replenishing what is lost.

The world has long treasured a certain gem, found in the pockets of sailors and travelers. Stories tell of fishermen who tucked it into their garments before venturing beyond the harbor, believing it held the calm and promise of clear seas. Merchants in bustling coastal markets kept it close at hand, exchanging it for spices or carrying it home in carved boxes as a charm against misfortune. In houses where the air smells of salt and driftwood, a single stone rests by the window, cool to the touch, a reminder of distant horizons and the steady return of the tides.

High in the mountains where snowmelt feeds the rivers, water weaves its way through rock and root. On bright mornings, villagers gather at the stream’s edge, washing their hands and faces before the day’s work begins. Children search along the banks for smooth pebbles, turning them over to catch glimpses of the sky reflected in their surfaces. At midday, workers dip their cups into the clear flow, drawing refreshment that seems to carry the memory of winter’s quiet and the promise of spring. These rivers shape the valley, nourishing fields and orchards, marking the rhythm of planting and harvest.

In the homes of skilled weavers, cloth is spun from threads carried over long distances, prized for its softness and sheen. Scarves and shawls, woven with care, drape over chairs or are tied loosely at the neck—gifts exchanged at weddings, blessings for journeys yet to come. A piece of this cloth, folded and tucked into a chest, becomes a legacy, passed from hand to hand, imbued with wishes for gentle fortunes and safe returns. In distant markets, travelers recognize the shimmer of the fabric and remember evenings by the riverside, when the air felt close and full of possibility.

Along the coast, cooks prepare soup from sea plants, simmering them in clay pots until the kitchen fills with the scent of brine and clean air. After illness or on the morning of a new year, a bowl is served to those who need renewal, its taste mild and restorative. In island towns, jars of preserved ocean delicacies sit on high shelves, to be opened on special occasions, each bite a quiet celebration of health and endurance. Such foods remind all who eat them that nourishment can rise from depths unseen, and that strength often follows rest.

When rain showers pass, the world sometimes wears a fresh clarity. Puddles gather in the hollows of stone steps, reflecting the open sky, while birds flutter back, shaking droplets from their wings. For a brief time, everything feels cleansed, the promise of new beginnings drifting on the air. Standing in this quiet after the storm, a person might feel the urge to breathe deeply and let go of the day’s worries, to watch the surface of the water as it settles into calm.

Today, you might keep a smooth gem in your pocket for reassurance, or prepare a pot of seaweed broth and sip it slowly in a quiet corner. Wrap a soft scarf around your shoulders as you step outdoors after rainfall, letting the fresh air settle your thoughts. Place a small pitcher of water beside your bed, as a silent vow to welcome hope each morning.
