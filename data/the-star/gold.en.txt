THE STAR ― Upright

The woman kneels at the water’s edge, surrounded by open air and quiet land. She holds two pitchers, pouring from each—one onto the ground, the other into the clear pool that mirrors the sky above. Over her, a single bright star shines with calm strength, surrounded by smaller companions. It is a still moment, a return of what was once lost. She trusts that what she pours into the earth will be accepted, and that what she pours into the water will not be wasted. This is the act at the core of hope: giving back to the world, certain there is renewal.

In distant times, people saw signs of this promise in the earth itself. Deep beneath the soil and stone, veins of a rare metal ran through mountains, hidden from the surface world. When discovered, these veins drew people in search of transformation. The metal, precious and heavy, was gathered from riverbeds by those who panned in patient circles, watching for the glint among ordinary pebbles. They followed its trail across continents—through forests, deserts, and over high ridges—each seeking a new beginning. Tales of lost cities built from this substance, or of hidden vaults beneath the hills, filled the dreams of travelers. Sometimes, the search led not to fortune, but to a deeper faith in returning: the belief that what is given to the earth will one day return, changed.

In the middle of bustling cities, the same material sits in the heart of commerce and ritual. Bankers move it across counters in compact bars, marks from distant lands pressed deeply into each side. Artisans hammer it into delicate shapes—rings, bracelets, and amulets—each piece meant to carry a wish or a memory. On festive holidays, a baker might hide a gleaming token inside a loaf, and the one who finds it is said to receive a year of luck. In old kitchens, battered trays handed from parent to child carry the traces of celebration and endurance, a daily reminder of lasting worth.

Nature, too, reflects this sense of revival. After a thunderstorm, light arcs across the sky, and stories travel: somewhere, at the end of the shining bridge, lies a hidden cache beneath soft earth. Farmers save the first harvest grain in a tiny sealed box, trusting that as the seasons turn, abundance will return. Along riversides, children sift through sand and gravel, hoping for even the smallest flake that catches the sun, a treasure that makes their hands feel blessed for a moment. Monks in quiet chapels polish their sacred vessels, believing that clarity and reflection honor what is most enduring.

Today, you might place a small charm beneath your pillow or tuck an heirloom ring into your pocket before stepping outside. You can share fresh bread with family, breaking it carefully to see if fortune waits inside. Let your hands catch the light on a well-worn cup, or pause beside a stream and watch for a glimmer beneath the ripples. In these gestures, the promise of return, of hope renewed, lives quietly.
