JUDGEMENT ― Upright

A horn shatters the hush above the clouds, its sound lingering over bare hills and distant rooftops. The call is not gentle—it is insistent, like a summons from a realm just beyond the waking edge. Below, coffins open, not with violence but by invitation, and people rise from their rest. Across the plain, figures stand, uncertain but compelled, faces tilted upward to the vaulted expanse. It is a moment of reckoning, yet also of vast possibility, as if the world has been swept clean by the sounding of that trumpet and is now waiting for new stories to be written upon it.

Throughout history and myth, this expanse above has been a canvas for change. In old Norse tales, the heavenly bridge arched overhead, guiding souls to destinations unknown. The Egyptians watched ibis birds cross the great dome on their yearly journey, reading omens in their flight. In the early hours, farmhands would gather at the edge of fields, eyes squinting upward, measuring the day’s promise by the openness above. The call to awaken, to answer, is as ancient as the world itself—carried on the same air as the voices of prophets and the laughter of children running through open courtyards.

Geography remembers this invitation. On the shores of the Mediterranean, fishermen mended their nets beneath the vast stretch above, trusting it to keep storms at bay until their boats could return. Shepherds on the Mongolian steppe looked for that same open reach before leading their flocks to water. In New Mexico, adobe homes were built with flat roofs, open to the elements, so that families could sleep beneath the stars and wake at dawn to the sweep overhead. From the Andes to the Himalayas, the space above has always been the place where prayers are sent and possibilities gather, a reminder that the world extends far beyond the walls of a single house or the edges of one small town.

In daily life, this promise is reflected in gestures both humble and profound. A clean sheet is spread across a sunlit bed, smoothed by careful hands, echoing the clarity above. Buckets are filled with cool water drawn from a well, the surface trembling with sky’s reflection, carried inside to quench thirst or rinse away the night’s dust. Newborns are lifted in strong arms, their faces turned upward, meeting the openness with wide eyes and quiet wonder. Sometimes, the gift is as simple as opening a window in early morning, letting the air move through every room, sweeping away what is no longer needed.

Today, a person can step outside and let the expanse above steady their breathing, answering the call to begin again. They might pin fresh laundry on a line, trusting the wind to finish what their hands have started. Writing a wish on a slip of paper and setting it beneath the open dome, they give their hopes to the same vastness that has received prayers for centuries. Or, standing by a basin of water, they can watch the trembling surface mirror the world’s invitation to rise and answer.
