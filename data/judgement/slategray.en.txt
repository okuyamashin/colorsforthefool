JUDGEMENT ― Reversed

On the card, figures rise from stone coffins to the sound of a trumpet, but in this moment, one figure lingers, half-upright, unsure. The call has rung out: a low, distant tone carried over the rooftops at dawn, echoing along rain-slicked streets and the flat roofs of old railway stations. The world waits, the air holding its breath. Above, the sky is the color of unfinished business, with clouds that press low and heavy, as if they, too, hover between movement and rest.

Far from the bright banners of celebration, there is the quiet patience of a weathered path—a footbridge crossing a river swelled by last night’s rain, its planks worn smooth by decades of footsteps. Iron rails line the bridge, stamped by years of drizzle and mist, a reminder of the steady passing of time. In the city, rooftops bear the steady hush of morning, their slate tiles cold from the night, resisting sun and storm alike. These roofs have seen promises made and broken, pigeons nesting in the eaves, the scuff of a worker’s boot as they pause, uncertain, to look at the horizon.

Beyond the city, the mountains rise with their craggy backs, the high slopes scattered with boulders that once found their way down in ancient, forgotten storms. The surface of these stones glimmers faintly in the dew—silent sentinels, unyielding, waiting for the day’s warmth. Below, in the valley, a low fog clings to the ground, neither rain nor clear air, an in-between world of blurred outlines and softened edges. Travelers pause at the trailhead; the path is there, but the hour is early and the weight of the journey settles on their shoulders.

Inside, in kitchens before sunrise, someone sets a vessel upon the stovetop—a kettle battered from years of use, its surface dulled by countless hands, waiting for water and flame. On the workbench, a whetstone rests beside a blade, its surface scored from patient sharpening, a tool that hovers between readiness and neglect. In the study, thick books sit unopened, their spines lined up in a row, dust motes drifting across covers that promise knowledge if only one will reach for them.

In gardens, the leaves of olive and sage, toughened by weather and time, bend beneath the weight of yesterday’s rain. Moss creeps between stones in the old well, soft yet persistent, marking the hours and the seasons. Here and there, among the stems, a feather fallen from a jackdaw or pigeon hints at recent flight. The air is cool and still; the world waits for a decision that has not yet come.

Today, take time to sharpen a favorite knife, remembering the slow rhythm of stone and steel. Consider walking a familiar path at dawn, pausing on a bridge or by a mossy wall to listen for distant bells or birdsong. Brew tea in the oldest kettle you own, letting steam rise in the quiet, and linger by the window to watch the fog shift and thin.
