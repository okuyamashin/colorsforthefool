JUDGEMENT ― Upright

Above the land, a horn echoes, clear and startling. Its notes tumble through the clouds, waking those who have lain silent and still in their heavy wooden boxes. The lids slip aside. Air—brisk and chill—rushes over faces that have waited for this call, faces that now turn upward. The moment is not gentle, but it is not cruel. It is simply necessary: a final recall to purpose, a shift from sleep to resolve. In this new light, the world is both familiar and utterly changed.

From this moment of rising, echoes travel not only through the sky but across ages and continents. In distant places, the horn’s call might be matched by the clang of a craftsman’s tools on a hard, pale surface—shaping smooth tusks into intricate combs or the handle of a favorite knife. There is a weight to this work, a sense of permanence. In ancient markets where traders assembled, such objects passed from hand to hand, carrying tales of long rivers, herds in the sun, and the quiet patience of those who carved the material into piano keys and chess pieces. Each finished item glowed with the promise of something enduring and rare, a testament to skill, patience, and the value of awakening what is hidden within.

In quiet rooms of old houses, the horn’s renewal is mirrored in the gleam of domed buttons on a wedding gown, kept carefully among linens. These treasured objects rest in cedar chests, their smooth surfaces holding memories of careful fingers fastening them before a day of vows. There is a hush in the room, as though the horn’s call has swept through the fabric itself, summoning old stories to rise into the present. The presence of these things—the carved figurine, the inlaid box, the key beneath a pianist’s hand—reminds us that transformation is not only grand, but can settle into small rituals. In their luster, we glimpse the gentle certainty of change, and the courage it takes to answer a call.

Weather, too, brings this moment near. There are mornings when fog covers the city, thick as fleece, muting every sound. It clings to windows and streetlamps, turning familiar corners into gentle mysteries. People step out into this quiet, as though reentering the world after a long sleep. The haze lifts slowly, and as it does, new shapes emerge: the steeple, the outstretched arm of a neighbor, the path ahead. In this quiet, the possibility of beginning anew is as tangible as the horn’s first note.

Today, you might take an old keepsake from its box and hold it in your hand, letting its smoothness remind you of skill and tradition. You may pause to write with a pen that feels cool and solid between your fingers, or choose the plate with a rim of delicate carving for your morning bread. If the weather brings a mist, walk quietly for a moment before the sky clears, listening for what might be calling you to rise.
