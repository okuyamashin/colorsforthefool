JUDGEMENT ― Upright

A horn rises above the clouds, its sound ringing over a landscape where stone covers slide aside and figures stand, eyes open to the sky. Each is called, not by fear, but by the unmistakable certainty of awakening. The world around them is filled with signs of renewal: the quilt folded at the end of a child’s bed, the morning’s first slice of angel food cake on a porcelain plate, the fine flour dusted over a baker’s board at dawn. In this atmosphere, the call of the horn is unmistakable, a gentle but undeniable nudge toward rising.

In ancient myth, the trumpet blast has always marked a crossing—Persephone’s return to the world of harvest, or the breath of spring that coaxes hyacinth and elderflowers from the earth’s quiet sleep. There is an echo of those rites in the pale lace curtains that billow by the open window, letting in the first day’s light after a long night. The feeling is of linens freshly washed and hung to dry, their scent mingling with the faint sweetness of almond blossom drifting from the garden. Such things are not loud proclamations; rather, they are invitations to notice, to step out, to heed the quiet summons of change.

Weather after a long storm, when mist lifts gently from orchard grass, holds this same quality—the hush that arrives just as the sun breaks through, outlining the world in subtle clarity. A tablecloth spread for an early breakfast, bread torn by hand and scattered with the last of the winter’s sugar, makes a kind of new beginning. The air itself seems to anticipate decisions gently now, not as a reckoning but as a welcome, a table set for all who rise. Even the ancient temples of the Mediterranean, their pillars worn smooth as bone by centuries of prayer and salt wind, gather this sense: a surface touched again and again by hope.

In the kitchen, the mixing bowl waits, rimmed with powder, ready for the next loaf or the next memory. The soft nap of a guest towel, folded and placed for someone expected, is another silent call. There is grace in these rituals, a readiness and a willingness to see what the day will offer. Each is a response to the horn’s sounding—a way of standing up, not to judgement’s dread, but to its invitation toward purpose and clarity.

Today, you might wake and open the curtains, allowing the gentlest light to touch your room. You could bake something simple, dusting the top with confectioner’s sugar, and eat it quietly to mark a new beginning. You might write a letter on thick paper, letting your words rise like morning bread. Or you could fold a fresh towel and set it in place for a guest, seen or unseen, as a gesture of welcome.
