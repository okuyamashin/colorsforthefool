JUDGEMENT ― Reversed

On the card, the awakened figures stand, reaching out to something that calls above them. Yet one soul, perched at the coffin’s edge, does not rise. The trumpet’s note fills the air, but action is postponed, the answer set aside for another day. The world waits around him, unmoved, heavy with intentions not yet realized. The ground beneath is neither grave nor open path. It is a pause, dense with memory.

In an old kitchen drawer, a piece of volcanic glass rests, its surface glossy and cold. Centuries ago, such a blade would have sliced through sinew or fruit, decisive and swift. Now, it waits in its shadowed compartment, unused but ready, the sharp promise of change dormant until someone chooses to wield it. The drawer is opened and closed, day after day, the obsidian always present but never called upon.

Far to the north, midnight covers a fjord. Clouds gather, thick enough to obscure the stars, and the air is hushed, expectant. The fisherman’s boats are moored, hulls rocking gently against the dock. Their nets and ropes remain coiled and untouched, silent testimony to weather that keeps the village indoors. The fishermen linger at home, sipping bitter drinks by the stove, uncertain if tomorrow the waters will be safer, if the sky will clear.

Below ground, in a tunnel carved by miners a century ago, seams of coal carve through the earth. The miners’ lamps flicker along damp walls, but the pickaxes are set aside, leaning against the entrance. The shift is postponed. The coal, pressed and ancient, lies undisturbed, its energy locked within. The darkness of the tunnel is total, patient, awaiting footsteps that will one day return.

In the house above, winter coats made of heavy velvet hang on pegs by the door. The velvet is soft, draping in folds that swallow the light, thick with the memory of evenings spent by the fire. Each coat waits for its owner to reach for it, to step into the cold. Today, the coats remain in place, the house undisturbed by departures or arrivals, the warmth inside unchallenged.

In the field behind, soil has been plowed and made ready. Rain has not come, and the farmer stands on the threshold, packet of seeds in hand. He looks at the earth, turns away, and sets the seeds by the window. The land is patient and so is he; the promise of harvest is delayed, but not forgotten.

This morning, one could bring the obsidian from its drawer and place it on the table, a reminder that intentions can become deeds. A walk outside beneath the thick skies might deepen the sense of waiting, a quiet companion to unfinished plans. The velvet coat could be taken down and wrapped around the shoulders, even if no one leaves the house. Or, with the seeds still by the window, one could simply hold them, letting their weight suggest a future decision.
