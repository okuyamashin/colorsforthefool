THE HERMIT ― Upright

A quiet path stretches upward, traced by the Hermit’s careful sandals. In one hand, he grips a wooden staff, smoothed by use and sleepless hours. In the other, a lantern: its glow does not reach far, but the warmth near the flame is steady, a circle of comfort against the chill. He is cloaked against the wind, each step measured, the world beyond the light withheld, as if even the sky is holding its breath. Solitude is not emptiness; it is a distillation, a trimming away so that only what matters remains.

In the Hermit’s journey, the lamp is not merely for vision, but for focus. The range of its glow is just enough to show the shape of the next stone, the branch that might catch a robe, or the sudden edge of the path. It is the concentrated radiance that a cook might seek when sifting flour for the first sponge cake of spring, or the softness of a kitchen towel laid gently over dough so it may rise undisturbed in the early hours. These rituals are quiet, attended by the faintest aroma of zest grated from a fruit, drifting through the air and settling into memory.

Far from the world’s bustle, there is a monastery high in the Alps where, before dawn, a single bell calls the brothers to prayer. In their refectory, a plate rests in the monk’s hands: a slice of cake, its crumb so light that it seems to float above the plate. The recipe is remembered from a distant grandmother, who once wrapped pastries in ruffled cloth and sent them with her children to market, the folds of fabric keeping the sweetness safe. Outside, the sun has not yet crested the mountain, but within, there is brightness enough to mark the page of a psalm book, the promise of a day beginning with small peace.

The Hermit’s way is not a highway, but a footpath through weather both fair and uncertain. There are days when the fog rolls across the hills so low the grass disappears, and the only sign of the trail is the shimmer on a spider’s web, or the way the mist beads upon last year’s straw left at the doorstep. Sometimes, in a quiet house, someone will draw the curtains for a while and let the morning lay its hush over the tablecloth, tracing a finger along the edge of a saucer, pausing before the first sip of tea. The world is large, but this room is just enough.

From myth comes the story of a weaver, patient and unseen, who threads her loom by a single candle’s glow. Her cloth is airy and fine, like the dresses carried in hatboxes to spring weddings. She works in silence so as not to disturb the sleeping household, knowing that the beauty in her task is quiet, the unfolding of care into something tangible. The Hermit knows this, too—the value of a single lamp, a plain staff, a sliver of cake broken and shared at the end of a long day.

Today, you might wrap a loaf in a soft napkin, or pause while grating a citrus fruit, letting the aroma linger on your hands. You could sit in the hush before sunrise and sip from a delicate cup, or gently smooth out the folds in a curtain, letting the first light tumble in. Each act, small and contained, holds the world at arm’s length, and brings its own comfort close.
