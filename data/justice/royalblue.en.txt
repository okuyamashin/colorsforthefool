JUSTICE ― Upright

The sword of Justice gleams, held unwavering above the scales, the blade carving a line between what is done and what will follow. The scales balance, minute and silent, their bowls catching the weight of cause and the echo of result. Behind, a cloth is suspended, not as a veil but as a boundary—soft enough to move with the wind, firm enough to divide what is seen from what is withheld. Noon light spills across the scene, direct and unfiltered, banishing ambiguity and shadow. In this hour, every action and its answer are visible at the same table, each guest recognized for what they bring.

There is a thread in this moment, running further than the cloth and sharper than the sword, connecting the present judgment to histories grand and small. The thread winds through the tapestries that once adorned the halls of kings, where lords in velvet mantles pronounced verdicts beneath banners. It whispers in the stone corridors of cathedrals, where justiciar and supplicant alike stared up at windows depicting ancient scenes of trial—archangels weighing souls, monarchs holding court, all beneath a sky clear as a summer’s open eye.

Crossing seas and centuries, this strand finds itself tangled in the rigging of distant ships. The mariners on their decks know the rules that keep them from chaos: the signal flag hoisted high as a promise of order, the sailor’s uniform, crisp and orderly, marking the division between officer and crew. Salt air scrubs everything clean, and the canvas stretches tight overhead, promising shelter but demanding discipline. Here, the midday sky is their arbiter, the horizon a perfect measure of what is owed and what is possible.

On far-off capes and islands, a rare flower blooms year after year beside ancient courts and city gates. Its petals hold a hue once reserved for those who judged or ruled, for the cloth that clothed wisdom and dignity. Even as seasons shift, the plant remains steadfast, a reminder that fairness takes root in unlikely places and endures through weathers both stormy and serene.

In quiet corners of the household, truth still seeks its emblem. The dinner plate passed evenly between guests, the tablecloth unfurled for all to share, the notebook turned so that both student and teacher see the lesson written in the same hand. The cup set at the same place each day honors the rituals that make fairness a habit. Even the raincoat, hung by the hearth after a midday storm, is a compact between shelter and exposure, law and chance.

Today, one might hang a banner outside the door, pledging openness to the honest exchange of word and deed. A book of laws, or simply a well-kept journal, can be set open on the table, its blank lines waiting for the day’s account. Tea can be poured, evenly, into two cups, and shared with a neighbor. A walk beneath the open sky at noon offers a moment to measure one’s heart against the calm and impartial expanse.
