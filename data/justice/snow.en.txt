JUSTICE ― Upright

A double-edged sword stands upright on a court table, its blade bright beneath the noon sun. The scales, precisely balanced, wait nearby. Between them, a heavy curtain hangs, dividing outer noise from inner calm. Light pours in, illuminating every surface where cause and result take their seats, face to face. In this room, nothing is hidden, not even the lingering drift from the world outside. Justice does not hurry, and so the measures remain patient; every action weighed with the silent certainty of something that has seen many seasons.

Beyond the court, in stories told across mountains, a silent blanket descends from the sky, covering rooftops and fields. Travelers in the far North recount mornings when the land is unbroken and new, the air sharp, and every branch wears a fine coat. On such days, footsteps are revealed—each print a record, impossible to hide or alter. The world grows quiet, but under that hush, everything is revealed: where someone went, what they carried, whether a bird landed or a hare passed by. Those who must judge or seek truth know to rise early, stepping outside before the tracks are blurred by wind or others’ passage.

In certain ancient towns, the weavers prepared their finest cloth, spun from flax in long winter months. These bolts were prized for purity and strength, reserved for magistrates’ robes or altar linings. They draped from windows at the first thaw, fluttering in the cold sun, signaling that the time had come to settle debts and disputes. The cloth held the memory of waiting, of patience rewarded and wrongs put right. You could find it in the judge’s square, on tables where contracts were signed, or folded on ledges, ready to mark a transition. In some places, it wrapped loaves or swaddled newborns, a silent promise that every beginning deserved candor and clarity.

In the kitchen, flour is scattered across the counter, the first step before bread is made. A baker’s hand moves with intent: too much, and the loaf is tough; too little, and the dough sticks, unworkable. Precision matters—no guesswork, no shortcuts. Looking out the window, the baker recalls seasons when the ground was covered, and the world seemed paused. These were the days when you could see your own path behind you, marked step by step, and so too in baking, every action leaves its trace. The bread that rises properly, crust shimmering, is a testament to right action and careful measure.

In the highlands, weather changes without warning. Once, messengers carried news across wide fields blanketed in the night's fresh gift, their path etched for all to see. The return journey bore the same marks, a silent record of truth delivered. Sometimes, the storms brought danger, but even then, the law of the land was clear. Shelter offered in harsh times was never forgotten; neither was harm done, for in this landscape, memory lingers in the open.

Today, you might hang a clean cloth in the window to mark a fresh start, or write what must be said on unmarked paper at noon, inviting clarity. You could bake a loaf with careful hands, ensuring every measure is fair, or step outside after a storm, letting each footprint remind you of actions that cannot be hidden.
