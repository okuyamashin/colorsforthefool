THE HIGH PRIESTESS ― Upright

Between two pillars stands the High Priestess, silent and composed. Her scroll remains folded against her lap, its script turned inward, withheld from the world. Behind her, a veil drapes from lintel to floor, stitched with pomegranate seed patterns, closing off the space beyond. The surface of water pools quietly at her feet, unbroken and clear, reflecting not the sky but the cluster of columns and cloth that enclose her. The air in this chamber is cool, and the hush is complete but for a faint wind sighing through a distant hallway.

The secret she keeps is not spoken. It lives in stone and paper, in the heaviness of still water, and in the memory of old slate roofs that shelter abbeys and libraries. These roofs, split and fitted by hand over centuries, stand through endless rains and mist, their surfaces slick with the hush of early morning. In the glen, the quarrymen once split fresh slabs, their hammers ringing against the layered earth, and each piece was lifted to crown churches, to mark the boundaries between sacred and secular ground. The roof tiles, seasoned by decades of tempests and gentle drizzle, are never just the color of rock; their sheen catches the first light after a storm and the last glow before dusk.

In the kitchen, quiet hours bring out another echo. The old breadboard, quarried from the same stone, cool to the touch and carved at the edges by years of use, holds the soft weight of plums and figs. Here, bread is sliced while the world outside is silent, and the crumb falls gently onto the stone. A mortar, turned from mountain slate, grinds seeds for morning porridge, each rotation a patience that matches the Priestess’s gaze. The patience in these acts, in letting the bread rise or the fruit steep, mirrors the patience needed to wait for a secret to unfold itself.

Far away, in a river valley where the air is always damp, the weather arrives in long, low clouds, veiling the land as thoroughly as the curtain behind the High Priestess. The fog rests on the hills, making the world smaller and quieter. Dew gathers on wild hyacinth and the petals of bluebell, their forms trembling just above the earth, hidden except to those who know where to look. The hush in the valley is the same hush that fills the temple, a breath held in the hour before revelation.

Not all secrets belong only to grand temples or distant landscapes. In a study lined with slate shelves, letters are kept in hidden boxes, their contents written in a careful hand. The shelves are smooth with time and touch, and the boxes close with a satisfying weight. A pen rests beside a sheet of thick paper, waiting for a thought that has not yet taken form. The quiet here is absolute, the only movement the faint shifting of light across the surface of stone.

Today, you can sweep your kitchen slate clean before preparing breakfast, allowing each movement to be steady and thoughtful. You can sit a moment longer by your old stone windowsill, watching the way the first light glances off its surface. You can write one secret hope in a notebook, closing the cover with deliberate care, or pause in the hush of early morning to listen for what stirs beneath silence. These small acts extend the stillness and patience of the High Priestess into everyday life.
