THE HIGH PRIESTESS ― Upright

Behind the High Priestess, two pillars rise in quiet contrast, framing a world paused at the edge of revelation. Between them hangs a veil, embroidered with fruit and subtle signs, its folds hiding the secrets of what lies beyond. She sits in calm, her feet resting upon the surface of still water. In her lap, a scroll marked with letters remains shut, the wisdom within waiting for the moment when one is ready to receive it. All is tranquil, yet charged with the promise of knowledge not yet spoken.

In the stories that echo through ancient halls, there are hidden springs, pools of water tucked beneath limestone cliffs. The surface of such a pool lies quiet beneath the overhang, reflecting the sky as though it were a piece of translucent glass. The air there is always cool, even in summer, with a mist rising early in the day and curling along the stones. In those secret places, water holds the memory of centuries, untroubled by footsteps, untouched by the sun except for those brief minutes when light slips through the rocks above.

Long ago, travelers crossing the high deserts of Asia paused at oases rimmed by wild sage and grasses. There, at dawn, the sky opened above the land, and a chill hung in the air before the day's heat arrived. Traders and pilgrims would drink from delicate porcelain cups—each one glazed in the tone of a distant sky, a color that came from careful alchemy and the patient hands of artisans. In these cups, steam curled above jasmine tea, joining the stillness of the predawn hush. The cups themselves were small, yet bore the mark of distant dynasties, reminders of knowledge carried in silence along the Silk Road.

In the home, there is a silk scarf folded neatly in a cedar chest, its weave fine and cool to the touch. The scarf once belonged to someone who valued quiet mornings and the practice of reading in solitude. It was worn draped over shoulders when the day was not yet begun, during the hour when dew gathered on windowpanes and the world felt hushed. There are stones, too, set into the frames of old mirrors and pendants—stones shaped by rivers and tumbled smooth by time. Some say such stones hold dreams, and that to carry one is to keep a secret safe, close to the heart.

The High Priestess holds herself at the threshold, neither within nor beyond. Her presence is like the early morning air of alpine lakes, where fog drifts along the surface before dissolving at sunrise. It is the hush of a day before anything is spoken, before the scroll is opened, when only the inmost intuitions are present. This is the atmosphere of a monastery courtyard after rain, when the world outside is washed clean, and the only sound is the gentle lapping of water against stone.

Today, one might begin the day by standing at an open window, breathing in the cool, damp air before dawn. Pouring tea into a porcelain cup, notice the condensation gather on its surface, and hold the warmth in your hands. Fold a scarf and place it where you sit, to remind yourself of patience and the value of waiting before speaking. Keep a small, smooth stone in your pocket or on your desk, and return to it when you need clarity or quiet.
