THE HIGH PRIESTESS ― Upright

Two pillars stand at the threshold, cool with the weight of silence. Between them, a veil hides what lies beyond, patterned with pomegranates, old as myth. At the feet of the High Priestess, still water catches the hush of breath and the promise of what is concealed. Nothing here is rushed; everything lingers at the edge of revelation. Her hands rest on a closed scroll, its secrets pressed tight and waiting. Her seat is stone, cool to the touch, carved by hands long gone, set in a temple where the air feels barely moved since the last dusk.

Beyond the veil, the story deepens. In the north country, light fog gathers on mornings when the ground is cool and the sun only a thought behind clouds. It moves in slowly, sliding over the fields and hedges, blanketing the world in a soft hush. Travelers passing by the old abbey see its towers rising pale and silent, their outlines softened by the low, rolling mist. The world is not erased but made more subtle, the details of things withdrawn just out of reach.

In a mountain pass, monks once walked to prayer before dawn. Their robes, worn threadbare through decades, had taken on the look of old linen left to dry in the cool air, woven and rewoven, every thread a testament to patience. The cloth was smooth, a touch lighter than parchment, folding quietly at each movement. They moved like shadows, purposeful and undisturbed, carrying baskets of rice flour to the kitchens, the powder rising in faint clouds as the baskets were set down.

In the kitchen, bread is sifted and kneaded. A ceramic bowl, cool and opaque, sits beside a pile of split garlic, the skin as thin as breath, curling away at the edges. A cook pours water for tea, and steam lifts, vanishing into the air, a brief, weightless curtain. In the far corner, a window reveals the orchard. In deep winter, plum trees wear a thin coat of rime, each branch outlined, sparkling in the first light that barely brushes the walls. On the windowsill, a folded letter rests, paper smoothed by a careful hand, its message sealed for now.

The secret is not gone, only resting. The High Priestess watches as a heron steps through the morning haze beside the temple pond, its feathers undisturbed by the world’s noise. She knows the language of thresholds—the patience of those who wait, of things not yet spoken. The veil does not forbid, but invites quietness, asks for one more moment before the scroll is opened. The hush is a space in which answers settle, not as thunder but as dew.

Today, someone might rise before dawn and sit by the window, listening for the first notes of morning. Another might steep a pot of tea, watching the steam fade and return. With a soft cloth, a person could polish the face of an old ceramic bowl, or take time to sift flour, letting each grain land in its own slow way. Each act is a way of honoring what remains hidden and what drifts just at the edge of knowing.
