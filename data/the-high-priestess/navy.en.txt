THE HIGH PRIESTESS ― Upright

Between two pillars, shrouded by a heavy curtain, the High Priestess keeps her secrets. She sits in a hush broken only by the slight ripple of water at her feet; what is written on the scroll in her lap stays unread, the script turned inward. The world here is patient and dense with meaning, just as a harbor waits at the mouth of the sea, fog rolling in to soften the shapes of distant ships. The mysteries do not rush forward. They rest behind the veil, waiting for the right time.

Picture the decks of old vessels, their timbers salt-worn and silent, crews moving in measured steps as the first lanterns are lit against the evening. The sea stretches out, deep and endless, a vast and unknowable library beneath the hulls. The horizon carries old stories: merchant fleets returning from distant realms, their holds full of spices and silk, their ledgers sealed, each journey a cipher. The sailors are bound by discipline and an understanding that the ocean gives nothing without exacting its due. There, as on the High Priestess’s card, discipline is paired with intuition. Knowledge is both a secret and a burden—a closed logbook, a locked chest.

In old ports, quayside warehouses stand as quiet sentinels. Their heavy doors conceal more than goods: records, receipts, the private notes of captains and customs officers. Even their uniforms, with their crisp lines and polished buttons, speak of order imposed on vast uncertainty. To pass through these places is to move through layers of ritual and protocol, much as a questioner approaches the priestess, only by invitation and never by force. The weather by the docks is often quiet, the air thick with a mist that dims noise and sight. Even in the rush of commerce, this is a place of stillness, where time pools in the corners and secrets gather unseen.

Back at home, the High Priestess may find her reflection in practical things: a tightly-bound ledger tucked into a drawer, its pages closed to idle eyes; the deep pot in which broth simmers quietly for hours, the lid never lifted until the moment is right; the weight of an old officer’s coat hung on a peg by the door, heavy with memories and rain. In each, the lesson is the same. The most important truths are not announced. They are kept, guarded by care and patience.

Today, a person might lock away a private thought in a journal, waiting until the right moment for it to be read. One might cook something slowly, refusing to peek, letting flavors deepen in the silence. A walk by the harbor or any quiet water gives space to let thoughts settle and secrets find their shape. Or, simply, one could arrange their desk or shelf, placing each item in order, preserving a private sense of balance and readiness for whatever may arrive.
