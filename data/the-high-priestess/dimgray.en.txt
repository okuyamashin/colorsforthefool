THE HIGH PRIESTESS ― Reversed

The scroll in the High Priestess’s lap remains unread, ink pressed forever between its papyrus leaves. The veil behind her, once a passage, has hardened into the stone of a city’s wall, indifferent to the questions asked on either side. Her pool, once shimmering, lies still beneath a sky of low clouds; the water’s surface offers no reflection, only a hush deep as winter fog. Where once a whisper might have crossed the threshold, now silence gathers itself, a shawl woven from the dusk of all unanswered things.

There is a house at the edge of the old quarter, its roof tiles dulled by years of rain. In the small garden, river stones border the path, each one worn smooth by uncounted steps. A cast-iron kettle sits on the stove, the same shade as the hearth’s ash after the last ember dies. The air holds the memory of nights when secrets were told, yet no voices come now, only the tick of the clock and the rustle of dry leaves outside. The world has the hush of early morning in winter, when the sky is close and the ground is quiet, and nothing moves but the thin smoke from a chimney.

Beyond the city, a mountain range rises, its faces veiled by morning mist. Peaks and valleys blend into one another, the outlines uncertain, the distance marked not by brilliance but by density, by the slow settling of old rain on ancient rock. Travelers pass along the ridge trails, boots coated in the dust of old roads, cloaks drawn tight against the chill that clings after a storm. The old miners knew this landscape well; their tools rest now in sheds at the foot of the hills, pickaxes and shovels embedded with the same dusk as the stones they once split open. Not every vein revealed itself, not every secret gave way.

At the river’s bend, willows lean toward the water, their bark damp and cool, their shadows stretching long. Fishermen, patient as the stone beneath their feet, mend their nets in silence, trusting the current but not the sky. The sun does not break through every day. Sometimes mist lingers until dusk, and even the wild pigeons gather quietly beneath the eaves, their feathers pressed close, their eyes watchful and still.

Inside the house, there is a woolen blanket folded at the foot of the bed, a remnant of journeys across moors and railway platforms. An iron key rests on the windowsill, its wards unturned for seasons now. The ledger on the desk lies closed, the pages thick with the dust of unresolved accounts. Above it, an old photograph in a pewter frame, faces turned away from the camera, eyes lifted toward something unseen beyond the frame. This is the hour when the unspoken weighs more than words; the secret exists, but it is a stone at the bottom of the well rather than a ripple on the surface.

To move within this space, one might polish the old key until its shape is revealed by touch alone, or make bread in the cast-iron pot, letting the quiet rise in the kitchen. Step out into the garden at dusk and arrange the river stones into a pattern known only to you, or walk beneath the clouded sky, listening for the stories that live in stillness.
