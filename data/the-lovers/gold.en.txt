THE LOVERS ― Upright

Two stand at the edge of a garden in the earliest light, a hush between them as the world waits. There is a tree heavy with fruit, a path curling through wild marigolds, and the hush is not absence but possibility. Their hands brush as they step together, not yet joined, not yet apart. The ground is soft where dew still clings, and the garden’s air carries the memory of what is planted and what will grow. Each choice is an echo. Something begins in the moment they look at each other and decide: not fate, but the slow work of choosing, the agreement in each gesture, each word.

In ancient rivers, people sifted through silt and sand for a glimmer, driven by the promise of what might be shaped into crowns, coins, or wedding bands pressed with the symbols of a family’s house. Kings sent expeditions to the mountains for it, where the sunlight struck rocks and veins threaded through stone, difficult to reach, impossible to forget. Once, in a city far from the river, an artisan hammered a delicate leaf from the precious metal and set it into the clasp of a lover’s cloak. The clasp was not just ornament; it was a seal, a commitment worn close, an object imbued with weight and promise.

On festival mornings in distant lands, bakers turn dough into sweet rings and brush them with a delicate layer of honey, then scatter strands of saffron so each loaf catches the light. These breads are baked for weddings or the feast days of saints who watched over unions. At the table, two people break the bread together, each tearing a piece to offer the other—the ritual binding them, the promise of sharing both hunger and plenty. The baker’s hand remembers old promises, the repetition of kneading and shaping, the satisfaction of something beautiful rising from ordinary grain.

At noon in the highlands, the sun can seem relentless, beating down until the fields shimmer. In those hours, shepherds lean on their staffs and watch as the sheep gather in the shelter of an acacia tree. The air is thick, and the world slows beneath the molten sky. In this intensity, choices must still be made: which path to the well, which lamb to carry, which shade to share. The heat presses close, and in its embrace, even the smallest decision feels weighty, a matter of trust.

In the city’s heart, a jeweler offers rings crafted from the same element once dredged from rivers, now spun into endless circles. Shoppers pause at the window, drawn to the promise of something enduring. The ring, cool at first touch, warms against the skin—a reminder that bonds, once chosen, are renewed each day. The weight of commitment is not a burden but a reassurance, solid and clear.

This morning you could bake bread and sweeten it with honey, offering the first slice to someone dear. You might press an old ring into your palm and remember the hands that gave it. Pause to notice marigolds in a neighbor’s yard, or slip a coin into the palm of a friend as a quiet sign of trust. Each act draws you closer to what matters, the beginning found in the choosing.
