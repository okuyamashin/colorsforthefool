THE LOVERS ― Reversed

In the quiet moment between, two stand together, hands close but not touching, words caught in the space where breath is felt but not shared. Around them, the world offers reminders of connections that could be, yet remain unchosen. In the old stories, there is always a crossroads where the path splits: Orpheus, reaching for Eurydice and turning too soon; lovers on the threshold of the underworld, called back by memory rather than hope. Here, the threshold is a kitchen table worn smooth by generations, its surface marked with the stains of stewed fruit and spilled coffee, the wood rounded by the resting of elbows during evenings when conversation once flowed.

The earth outside is dry, summer-dusted, the color of almond skins and cured leather left to age in the sun. In the market stalls of distant towns, baskets of late-season figs stand beside loaves of bread whose crusts are thick and cracked, a sign of patient labor and the heat of a slow oven. The scent of cinnamon lingers from a jar left open, mingling with the faint aroma of roasting chestnuts, each shell split and cooling on an old iron tray. These are the tastes and textures that should be passed from hand to hand, but instead remain untouched, their warmth fading on the table.

The mountain ranges on the horizon recall ancient trade roads, where merchants guided their donkeys along winding tracks, exchanging dates and soft wool for copper pots and woven carpets. In the morning, mist sometimes rises over these ridges, settling in the valleys like a shawl drawn over tired shoulders. Yet as evening falls, the wind brings a dry spell, air thick with dust and the promise of rain that never comes. Underneath the eaves, a bundle of dried roses hangs upside down, petals curled inward, saving what fragrance they can. The garden’s old clay pots, once filled with sweet peas, now hold only remnants of last year’s harvest, husks and brittle stems.

Inside, a pair of gloves rests on a low stool—suede worn smooth at the palms, saved for autumn walks that never happened. There is a coat draped over the arm of a faded armchair, its lining patched where it once tore on a bramble. These are the markers of intentions set aside, gestures begun but not finished. The silence in the house grows as evening deepens, punctuated by the soft click of a latch that was not locked, an invitation unanswered.

To move gently through this day, one might place a handful of dried figs in a small dish, letting their sweetness linger on the tongue. Another could take a moment to oil the wooden table, running a cloth in slow circles to return some sheen to its surface. Gathering fallen rose petals from under the old bundle and tucking them into a pocket might bring the memory of summer closer. Or, pausing by the window to watch dust rise and settle in a beam of late sunlight, someone might remember the patient work of mending, and pick up a needle to repair the lining of a coat left waiting.
