THE LOVERS ― Upright

Two figures stand in a well-tended garden. Birds sing above their heads, and the air is quiet except for the gentle movement of leaves. The earth is soft beneath their feet, and the warmth of the afternoon sun lies on their shoulders. Here, the act of choosing is not a hurried gesture, but the first stone laid in a path stretching forward together. The space between them fills with trust, and the very trees seem to bend closer, listening for their decision.

The garden they walk is old, cultivated with care. There are clusters of delicate blooms, each unfurling petals the same shade as the shell of a conch found on a faraway shore. In history, such flowers—delicate and fragrant—were gathered before dawn, pressed into garlands for new lovers or scattered along a wedding path. As in myth, where a goddess handed a bough of these blossoms to the one who would choose her, the giving of such a simple thing is a promise of return, a quiet entwining of destinies.

Elsewhere, the fruit of a certain orchard is prized not only for its sweetness but for a skin as soft as the lining of a seashell, prized in markets from Anatolia to the markets of the Far East. Once, a trader sailed from island to island, baskets of these fruits beside him, each one a token for an awaiting heart. On festival days, these treasures were sliced and arranged on delicate plates, offered from one hand to another as a sign of goodwill, kinship, or the start of something daringly new.

Consider the thread drawn from the cocoon of a small moth, spun into cloth by the hands of generations. Garments fashioned from this fabric have swirled around dancers at spring feasts and covered the shoulders of bridal parties in villages nested among wild rose thickets. The touch is always gentle, a reminder of how the softest things last longest when cherished. In other times, tailors would cut tiny ribbons from this cloth, tying them to branches at crossroads or threading them into the hair of those about to take a vow.

High in the mountains, just as the day breaks, the sky sometimes takes on the shade seen in the petal of a blooming camellia, a promise of clear weather and gentle warmth. Shepherds watching their flocks would pause to watch the light shift and know that the day ahead would be kind.

The choice that begins the bond is not only a private act but one woven through the things we grow, make, and share. Today, you might place a garden bloom on your table, or offer a small fruit to someone you care for. You could tie a ribbon from a forgotten spool around your wrist, or take a morning to watch the sky's changing face, letting the moment's calm direct your next step. Each gesture draws you quietly closer not only to another, but to the ongoing, ancient story of joining.
