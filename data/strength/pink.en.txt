STRENGTH ― Upright

The garland of wildflowers rests across her hair, a simple coronet pressed by the gentle weight of petals. Her steady hand closes the lion’s jaw with a calm that soothes muscle and mane, patient and humble. The card’s presence is silent and enduring, as if echoing a garden at dawn, where dew laces the heads of carnation and peony, and even the fiercest creature pauses before the hush of morning. In the language of myth, such encounters are not battles but quiet compacts—Heracles taming the Nemean beast not with violence but with a promise, tales where difficult tasks are met with soft resolve, and the defeated roar dies beneath the touch of composure.

Walk the stone paths of Kyoto in spring, and you’ll find another emblem of this force made gentle. Along the Philosopher’s Walk, camellias and moss embrace the old canal, and fruit trees linger in their brief moment of abundance. The petals drift down, blanketing the world in silence, unhurried and stubborn in their insistence to be noticed without clamoring. In medieval gardens, women would twist the heads of oleander and clusters of sweet roses into circlets, a sign of modest triumph at a time when armor was forged for quieter wars. These flowers traveled continents in the trunks of explorers, pressed flat into journals, proof of a world that could be carried with patient hands and a little faith in new soil.

The story returns indoors, to kitchens where dough is rolled for sweet loaves, the flour-dusted apron pressed and tied by a ribbon. Beside the bread rises a dish of candied petals, the same that once crowned the heads of festival dancers. In the afternoon, children gather on weathered benches, biting into slices of fruit flesh tender as the dawn’s first light, laughing as the juice stains their fingertips. In drawing rooms, artisans work with fine threads, embroidering handkerchiefs that once belonged to grandmothers, each stitch a hush, a wordless memory, a mark of labor neither loud nor ostentatious.

There are places, too, where the sky itself becomes an emblem of this silent strength. In coastal towns after a night of rain, the morning fog lifts and the horizon glows with the soft promise of a clear day. Fishermen ready their boats as the clouds retreat, a gentle weather that makes no boast of its change, yet invites the world to begin again. Above high desert mesas, clusters of wildflowers—mountain laurel and oleander—cling to stone, defying wind and drought, a reminder that resilience wears its softness openly and is never loud.

Today, a person can gather a bouquet of garden asters or carnations, and let their scent fill the morning air. Try baking a small cake and decorating it with sugared petals, offering a slice to a friend in quiet gratitude. Arrange heirloom linens on the breakfast table, tracing the needlework and recalling the hands that worked each careful thread. Walk early beneath fruit trees, and pause to notice the hush when the breeze moves through the blossoms, a force that shapes the world without a sound.
