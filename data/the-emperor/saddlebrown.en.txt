THE EMPEROR ― Upright

A throne rises from the earth, not gilded or hung with silks, but heavy and lasting, cut from the trunk of history itself, thick and patient as a seasoned yoke. The Emperor sits, boots planted on a worn flagstone, as if he has always been here. His arms, strong as harness leather, rest upon the arms of his seat. Behind him, the border is a low, dry fence built of old beams, marking what is his, splitting land from wild. Beyond: mountains, bare and unyielding, standing in ranks like cattle at the edge of a pasture. The wind here is a dry current, the sort that carries the scent of earth and old timber, and the memory of every road carved by cartwheels and hooves.

Order begins at the gate, a barrier made from posts set deep into the ground. The posts are carved by weather and time, but they hold. The Emperor’s law is not only written but hammered and shaped, echoing in the rails of a corral, the haft of a tool, the grain of a hand-stitched saddle. Civilization, in its earliest sense, takes shape in these things. The seat of power is not a fragile thing, but a settler’s bench, broad as the back of an ox, shaped by many hands, smoothed by generations sitting and rising, settling quarrels and setting plans.

The mountain’s roots are the Emperor’s roots, patient and resisting the push of storm. Below the peaks, ancient tracks snake through the dust, rutted by wagon and mule. Along these tracks, the world’s work is done: the trading of wheat for tools, the leading of sheep from one valley to another, the return to a house where the hearth is stoked with the remains of last year’s wood, saved and stacked before the snows. In the kitchen, the morning loaf is shaped on a broad board, its surface scored with the marks of the knife, and beside it, a spoon is carved from heartwood, polished by use. Above the stove, an old hat hangs, brim curled from years of rain and sun, always set back in its place.

The Emperor’s realm is measured not in fleeting splendor but in the constancy of what endures. The walls of his house are paneled with planks, each one fitted with care, their knots and scars held together with hand-forged nails. On the table sits a mug, handle thick, fired in the old way, passed from father to son. A recipe is read aloud, the words worn but remembered, a procedure followed the way a boundary is respected: not for show, but for the keeping of peace. The Emperor knows each shovel in the shed, each latch that must be lifted just so, each patch of earth that will yield best to the plow. Evenings arrive cool, with clouds gathering on the horizon, promising rain or perhaps not, but the road home is always marked by the line of pasture fence, never lost.

Today, you can oil the hinges on the garden gate, or mend the stitching on a favorite pair of boots. You might shape bread dough by hand, remembering how each fold matters, or polish a tool, steadying your mind with the weight of a handle. There is luck in the rituals of order, in keeping boundaries clear and procedures steady.
