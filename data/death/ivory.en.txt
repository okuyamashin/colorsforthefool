DEATH ― Upright

A rider pushes forward at dawn, his gloved hand carrying a flower with six gentle petals, swaying lightly in the new air. It is not the chill of night, nor yet the burn of noon, but a space in between: the last mist lingering over fields, the stillness that comes before the city wakes. Between two towers, the sky holds its breath, and the rider’s horse stamps quietly where grass meets stone. The end of one chapter becomes the silent margin for another.

There is something in the rider’s hand that once belonged to an ancient animal, roaming the banks of distant rivers. When the beast fell, its long curved tooth was unearthed by those who shaped its surface into the keys of a piano or the grip of a letter opener, smooth beneath the thumb. Centuries ago, these tusks became the tusks of empire, bearing the weight of trade and the shadow of extinction. Yet here, in the symbol of the flower, that old bone is transformed into something gentle, the memory of an ending turning softly toward renewal.

The towers at the horizon resemble the gates of old cities—places where stone lions once guarded the boundary between the past and the unknown. Inside such gates, merchants have measured out grains of rice with scoops carved from the same precious material as the chess pieces waiting on a board. In narrow kitchens, a cook stirs rice pudding in a shallow bowl, the grains swelling and softening, a meal both plain and ceremonial, fitting for the close of a season. From the domes of faraway pagodas to the handles of brushes used for painting calligraphy, this material has marked the careful crossing from death to life, from what is finished to what is just beginning.

If you step outside into the early morning, the air is as gentle as the dust brushed from the surface of a piano’s key. The sunlight is not yet sharp, filtered by thin clouds—a weather that invites quiet change. At home, you might find a comb resting on your dresser, carved from a single piece, a tool for smoothing hair before a new day. Or you might pause by a window, watching the softness in a petal that has fallen onto the sill, its texture promising the return of spring after the hush of winter. The outline of a door, newly painted, hints at the passage from one room to another, unnoticed by most and yet a true threshold.

Today, you can place a chess piece on the board alongside an old friend, letting the click remind you that every move carries an echo of things that have ended and things that may unfold. You may stir grains in a bowl, turning them with care as you think of the hands that shaped the scoop you use. You can open a letter with a knife made for this small ceremony, and let the act invite the possibility of new messages. If the weather allows, leave a window cracked as you sleep, so that the gentle air may slip in at dawn and remind you that each ending brings in something quiet and alive.
