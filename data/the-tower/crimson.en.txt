THE TOWER ― Upright

A flash splits the sky, and the tower is struck. In that instant, stone cracks, the crown falls from the spire, and everything kept within is forced into the night air. The storm rages, and what the walls once protected is scattered across the ground. Secrets, long hidden under the weight of tradition and pride, are unmasked by a force that cares nothing for human boundaries. The shock is sudden, irreversible; inside and outside trade places as the fabric of safety is torn away.

Long ago, travelers and traders scoured distant lands in search of tiny insects clinging to the prickly pads of cacti. From these beetles, they coaxed a brilliant substance that would be carried in sealed jars across seas and deserts. In royal courts, it stained the robes of judges and the banners of victory, reserved for the moments when power had to be declared unmistakable. For centuries, only a few knew the origin of that mark; the guild doors stayed closed, and the secrets of extraction and dyeing were guarded by oath. But the world changes when the walls come down, and these mysteries, once locked away, spilled into the open markets, carried by wind and rumor.

Near ruined temples and fallen palaces, the pomegranate tree stands. Its bark is rough, its branches twisted by years of sun, but its fruit, when split open by hand or by the force of weather, reveals a chamber of glittering seeds. Once hidden beneath thick skin, the arils spill out into the light with a burst. In ancient tales, these seeds marked the crossing from captivity into revelation, from shadow into consequence. To taste them is to remember the price of emergence, the bargain struck when what is confined is finally let out.

Velvet, woven thick and soft in the workshops of Anatolia, journeyed with caravans beneath storm-heavy skies. Behind closed doors, dyers worked late, their hands stained for days, their craft a mix of patience and risk. Velvet, once forbidden or reserved for those who held the keys to the city gates, became the sign of new beginnings when restrictions collapsed. Tailors and rebels alike cut and shaped the fabric into cloaks for a changed world, wearing the sign of transformation across their shoulders.

Elsewhere, the kitchen offers its own sudden revelations. A sharp knife meets a late-winter fruit, and the juice flows, marking the wood. The kitchen’s quiet is broken for only a moment, but the memory lingers. What was sealed inside bursts out, unmistakable and lasting. After the collapse, the old order cannot be put back together. The fallen crown lies in the rubble, and the air is charged with something that cannot be ignored.

Begin your day by scattering pomegranate seeds onto your oatmeal, remembering that what is hidden will one day show itself. Tie a velvet ribbon at your wrist, a quiet nod to the courage of new beginnings after collapse. Brew a cup of hibiscus tea, sipping slowly as you let memory and revelation mingle. When lightning flashes or thunder rolls, open a window and let the storm air in.
