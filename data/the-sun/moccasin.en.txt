THE SUN ― Reversed

Overhead, the light comes down hard, flattening all the edges of the landscape. In the world of the card, the child on the horse is squinting beneath an endless blaze; what once felt warm has become relentless. The banners hang as if parched, and the flowers tilt their heads, etched in sharp relief against a sky with no shade. It is a day when the road stretches long and undisturbed, sand swirling up around the ankles, each step pressing forward in dry, stubborn perseverance.

On such a day, every journey is felt in the feet. The land is dry, the grasses brittle. The old ones remembered to stitch coverings for the soles, shaped to cradle the foot, flexible enough for the shifting dunes and the uneven woodland floor. These shoes are a memory of riverbanks and animal trails, made from the hide of creatures who once traveled the same paths. Each stitch fastened with sinew, soft enough to fold in a pack, strong enough to endure the endless walking. They are worn by hunters in the birch forests and by traders along the rough trails, a tool born from necessity where the earth itself burns through to the skin.

In the stories of the wide plains, people gathered around fires beneath enormous skies, where dryness was a companion for weeks on end. Between journeys, there was a kind of ritual: dust brushed from the shoes, every crease and mark a record of stone and thorn. In homes lined with woven mats, a pair would be set by the door, their presence a quiet sign of someone returned safely from the open land, or a silent promise of a journey yet to come. If you look closely, you can see in each surface the memory of a long walk—creases where the foot bends, smoothness from the rubbing of sand, the shape of someone’s steps pressed deep into the leather.

Myth speaks of the road as both a teacher and a test. In some tales, a hero is given a pair of these shoes at the start of a quest, a token of protection against the heat, the rocks, the thorns. There is a wisdom in their simplicity: to shield the feet but never to separate the traveler from the earth. The fit and the feel carry the marks of journeys beneath an unyielding sun, where every mile is earned and remembered.

Even now, there are quiet ways to honor this sense of endurance. One might take a pair of soft, well-made slippers and step outside to feel the pathway underfoot, remembering the long roads that connect home to horizon. A small brush can tend to the shoes by the door, brushing away old dust and readying them for a new walk. A piece of old, supple hide can be sewn into a pouch, holding seeds or coins, a private keepsake of journeys made and journeys yet waiting.
