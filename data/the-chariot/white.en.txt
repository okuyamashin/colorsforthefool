THE CHARIOT ― Upright

The old arches of the city gate cast a shadow across the paved road, where a pair of harnessed creatures, their manes brushed and their hooves sharp, stand restless. From beneath a canopy stiff with linen, the driver leans forward, hands steadying the reins. Street dust clings to the folds of his tunic and to the bristles of a broom propped against the marble steps nearby. The city breathes behind him, crowds ebbing and flowing, the first notes of dawn moving like a slow tide between pillars and porticos. Outside the gate, the world stretches—a road that promises arrival if the chariot’s reins are met with skill and will.

Equipped for the journey, the charioteer checks the bundles at his feet: a loaf, wrapped in a napkin, and a jug with the cool clarity of morning water. The beasts shift, stamping the hard ground, their breath rising in clouds that dissolve in the clean, early air. Beyond, in the orchard lands, the almond trees are in bloom, petals scattering to join others along the roadside. Each petal a reminder—travel is forward, but also a gathering of what falls away. Overhead, the sky is blank and empty, a field waiting for the imprint of birds or banners.

Myths echo in the architecture of this morning. Warriors of old, astride their chargers, carried standards of woven wool, shields bright with the chalk of the quarry. In the distance, shepherds rise in their cottages, folding cheese into cloth slings for the day’s labor, their charges stirring in straw. The market in the city’s heart wakes with the shush of rice poured into bins, and the silent weight of salt slabs stacked in the stalls. The air carries a promise—each thing in its place, each motion precise, as if the world is made for progress when order is kept.

The charioteer exhales, his mind turning to the discipline of forward movement. In his home, he keeps a bar of soap by the basin, turning it in his hands before the journey, the lather chasing away the dust and fatigue. This is a ritual, as much as the preparation of the beasts or the careful knotting of the reins. Above the city, on the high hills where the wind is keen, a monastery stands, its bell ringing through the mist. There, monks rise to tend the parchment leaves of their manuscripts, each sheet pressed and dried with patience, each morning begun with a sweep of their simple brooms.

The day grows brighter, and the road calls. Before setting out, a person can sweep the doorstep, clearing the path for the day. They can pour cool water into a clear vessel, pausing to sip before the work ahead. They might wrap their midday meal in freshly laundered cloth, or knot a linen handkerchief for the journey. These are quiet preparations, pairing what is at hand with what is to come, steadying the mind for forward motion.
