THE FOOL ― Reversed

A dog snaps at the heels of a traveler who is one step away from the edge. The knapsack bobs but the road is only half-formed, the first stones in the path winking out beneath the traveler’s foot. A feather, an echo of lightness, sits behind the ear, but the wind is restless and heavier than it first felt. The crowd below is invisible, the cliff’s end a rumor. This is the moment the leap overtakes the landing.

In old stories, the first step off certainty often leads into a well, deep and echoing, lined with stones smoothed by years of hidden water. Fables warn of the pit and its quiet, where daylight cuts off and the voice rings back to itself. Travelers once cast pennies or pebbles into the well to measure its depth; sometimes a coin would vanish with no sound, sometimes it would clatter and settle among lost rings and forgotten keys. Down there are seeds waiting for spring, roots using silence as shelter, and the secret sleep of creatures that do not worry about dawn.

Far from the village, there are caves formed from the slow patience of rain and time, mouths that open under tangled roots or behind mossy boulders. Some have held monks who sought the hush for clarity, others offered thieves a place to count spoils. The air in these hollows is cool and still, holding the fragrance of earth, old fire, and the memory of footsteps that entered with one question and often left with another. To enter is to agree to uncertainty, to feel the pulse of the hillside against your back, to listen for water that moves where the sun cannot reach.

In kitchens, there are stones that have been polished smooth by hands, mortars that grind spice and grain, bowls for fermenting dough overnight. Bread rises in the silent hours, covered against drafts. Cast-iron pots, heavy with time, hold stews that change flavor as they simmer, lid closed to the world. The same quiet strength steadies a needle threading through dense fabric, stitches holding memories and mending what was torn. There is patience woven into every tool that waits out of sight until a hand reaches for it with need rather than habit.

On the coast, dawn sometimes brings a weather that swallows the horizon. Fishing boats tie up and wait out the change, nets coiled and lanterns stilled. The world is muffled, outlines softened, and the sea’s voice is the only clear thing. The air presses close and refuses to promise what will come next. For those who have miles to walk, a coat hung on the door blocks the gusts that sweep in. Inside, ink flows over a page, carrying messages born in the hush between storms.

You could drop a stone into the nearest well and listen as long as the echo takes to return. You could find an old mortar and grind seeds, remembering the strength of slow hands. You could simmer lentils in a heavy pot, letting the hours change their taste. Or you could open your window when the thick weather settles, and let your thoughts move through it, patient as roots in the dark.
