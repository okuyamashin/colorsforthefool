THE FOOL ― Upright

The wild rose rests gently in his palm, and a small dog dances at his side, both poised at the edge where cultivated earth meets open air. Beneath his foot, the short, springy lawn offers a foundation—soft yet reliable—for a step into the unknown. The grass underfoot is freshly clipped, cool with the last of the morning dew, and dotted here and there with clover and scattered petals from yesterday’s breezes. All around, the grounds hum quietly: a gentle patchwork of blades, shaped by days of care, stretching from garden border to shadowed hedge.

The beginning of any adventure is marked by such spaces, where the earth is even and unbroken by hardship, inviting the first stride. In village greens across history, games once began here—cricket matches set up in the afternoon, or children racing barefoot from one end to the other, their laughter trailing over the clipped surface. Out on the city commons, neighbors gather for early evening strolls, shoes brushing over turf that’s still cool from a sudden shower. Suburban mornings bring the steady drone of the mower and the scent of mown grass drifting in through open windows, mingling with the aroma of coffee and toast. On grand estates, the butler might once have announced garden parties or outdoor teas, the guests stepping carefully onto the close-cropped lawn, each blade gleaming after a rain.

The Fool’s confidence is nurtured by these familiar grounds: the soft resilience beneath, the rhythm of the caretaker’s rake, the trust in what lies just below the surface. Early in the day, the grass shimmers with droplets, brushing against ankles and paws alike. Gardeners lean on spades at the end of their work, and children lie flat, watching clouds drift overhead. Along the borders, mint and thyme run wild, their scent mixing with earthy roots as rainwater trickles through the channels left by spades and trowels. Evenings bring families onto quilts and folding chairs, sharing stories while the cool ground cushions their weight.

Throughout the world, lawns have gathered communities—picnics on tartan blankets, croquet mallets striking with a thud, or the quiet attention of those trimming edges with patient hands. Japanese landscapes are shaped by careful shears and reverence, while city parks echo with footsteps and the sound of distant celebration. Through sudden showers or gentle dawn, the grass endures, waiting for anyone willing to start their own journey from its level expanse.

To bring this spirit forward, one might kick off shoes and walk slowly across the clipped grass, noticing the subtle give beneath every toe. Reading beside a bed of herbs, sharing a simple sandwich outdoors, or tending the corners of the yard with quiet care can all offer a sense of renewal. Gathering flowers, letting rain wet your feet, or kneeling to pull a weed ties today to the steady promise of new beginnings.
