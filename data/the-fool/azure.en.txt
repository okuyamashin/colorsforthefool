THE FOOL ― Upright

He stands at the brink, the soft bud of a rose held gently between his fingers, while a small dog dances at his heels, urging him onward. The earth falls away just ahead, but his step remains light, as if the air itself will carry him forward. The road is not yet marked, the world not yet mapped; only the whisper of a breeze and the weightless morning accompany him. With a knapsack slung over his shoulder—inside it, a crusty roll from yesterday, a folded cloth napkin, a polished pebble—he greets the journey with an open heart. The day stretches wide before him, as infinite as the great arch overhead.

In the tales of old, travelers and dreamers alike have paused at such a threshold, looking up for guidance in the shape of the sky. Ancient mariners, before setting sail from the harbors of the Aegean, lifted their faces to the boundless expanse above, trusting the clarity above to promise fair passage. On the Silk Road, caravan leaders rose before sunrise, reading the heavens as an invitation to begin the day’s crossing. Himalayan villagers, living at the world’s rooftop, wake to a hush that reminds them of all that is possible when the world is fresh and clear. Across continents, the sky serves as a sign for beginnings—a lidless invitation to step into the unknown.

In the city, morning finds its own ritual. Bakers rise while others sleep, dusting their counters and rolling dough, the first loaves cooling by open windows as the world brightens. On washing lines, families hang their linens and shirts before breakfast, letting them flutter and breathe in the gentle air. Glassware, newly cleaned, glimmers on sills, reflecting shapes that shift and dance with the changing light. There is a freshness to these actions, a sense of possibility, as if each object—cloth, bowl, or carafe—carries the secret of starting anew.

The Fool’s gift is this: to carry no burden but hope, to see every threshold as a place of promise. In legend, those who embarked without fear often found that the world responded in kind, offering serendipity at every turn. Japanese fishermen, preparing for their daily venture, offer prayers at dawn, believing that the expanse above will keep their nets full and their hearts light. In quiet meadows, children run barefoot through grass still wet with morning, inventing stories that float just beyond the horizon.

Today, you can welcome the spirit of this open journey. Open your window at first light and breathe deeply, letting the early air refresh your thoughts. Set a jug of water in a bright place, and watch as its surface catches the shimmer of a waking world. Smooth a piece of linen or cotton on your table, dedicating it as a space for a new idea or a handwritten note. Or step outside, even briefly, and let your eyes rest on the distant vault, allowing yourself a moment of wonder at how much is still ahead.
