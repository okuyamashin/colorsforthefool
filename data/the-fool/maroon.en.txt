THE FOOL ― Reversed

A figure stands at the cliff’s edge, bundle in hand, eyes on the sky, unaware of the stony ground below. The dog at his heel yips in warning, but the Fool’s step hovers in the air, trusting in something unseen. The card, upside down, holds its breath at the moment before the fall—when lightness turns into a misstep, and the journey’s promise tilts toward risk. The air is thin here, the wayward traveler outpacing caution, skipping the check that anchors the heart to the earth.

Below, a world teems with signs of what happens when one leaps before looking. On Caribbean islands, high in the hills where fugitives once hid, settlements grew out of flight. These were places built by those who ran from ships and chains, who risked the dark forest’s embrace before the path was clear. Villages thrived beyond the mapped land, their stories woven into the soil. The freedom found there was hard-won and uncertain, a fortress in valleys no one else dared to chart. Their kitchens, lined with heavy, seasoned pots, simmered with the scent of beans stewed for hours, thick with cloves and smoke, a meal born of what was gathered rather than what was planned.

Elsewhere, in quiet studies, a leather-bound journal lies unopened atop a writing desk, its cover worn smooth by hands that never quite settled to the task. The pages inside are blank, waiting for a steady pen, but the temptation is to start mid-sentence, to write without knowing what came before. At the hearth, a drying rack holds a woolen scarf, the kind knit for long journeys on uncertain roads. Loops and knots mark the places where the yarn slipped or doubled back, a map of mistakes that warms the neck despite its flaws. In gardens tucked behind stone walls, pomegranates split open in the heat, seeds tumbling across the flagstones, never destined for neat rows or careful plans.

There are days when the wind turns sudden, carrying the sharp scent of rain. The sky grows low, promising a storm that may pass by or break overhead. The wise shut windows and bring tools inside, but the Fool, reversed, would walk out into that weather, arms wide, ignoring the thunder that rolls through the valley. At sea, an old ship’s hull, painted in a deep, solemn hue, rocks against its mooring, waiting for the tide to shift. There is history in that timber, marked by the salt and the sun, patched and tarred where it threatened to split. Sometimes a passenger leaps for the rope before the dock is close, feet finding slick boards and splinters instead of safe ground.

Today, find an old pot and cook a one-pot dish from what you have, letting the flavors merge in their own way. Pick up a scarf—worn or new—and wrap it close before stepping outside, trusting it to keep you warm on an unplanned walk. Begin a letter or a journal entry, not at the start but wherever your hand rests, letting the story unfold without a plan. If the weather shifts, open a window just enough to catch the first scent of rain, then close it and light a candle, remembering those who took risks when the outcome was unknown.
