THE FOOL ― Upright

At the edge of a high path, a traveler pauses with only a rose in hand and a cheerful dog circling his boots. The air is bright and cool, alive with the promise of a new chapter. Each step forward is light, trusting, and a little reckless, as if the very earth is gently urging him on. Around him, the world is full of living things that speak quietly of beginnings—leaves unfurling, stalks standing tall, and the scent of cut stems rising from the ground.

Near the first bend in the trail, fresh shoots of wild mint crowd the banks of a small stream. Their crisp aroma floats upward, mingling with the dew as a fisher kneels nearby, using a basket woven from new willow stems. Along the water’s edge, scattered clusters of watercress bob in the gentle current, their round leaves trembling with every eddy. The hiker stops to gather a handful, feeling the coolness soak into his palm before tucking the greens into his pack. The world feels young, open, and impossible to break.

In the story of a wanderer in old Japan, the hero seeks a rare herb said to restore lost energy. He follows a winding path between rice paddies and mossy stones, guided by the call of a distant cuckoo. At the far end of a marsh, he finds it—a spiky sprig growing amidst the tangled grass, close to where frogs hide beneath the damp, glossy undergrowth. The herb is clipped carefully with a folding knife, wrapped in a scrap of cloth, and carried home as a treasure. This ritual, repeated year after year, marks the hopeful start of every growing season.

In a village in Provence, the first cutting of garden cress is a tradition. Children run barefoot through kitchen gardens, plucking the newest, most delicate leaves to sprinkle over bowls of soup. Their laughter mingles with the sound of gardeners drawing water from the well and the soft brushing of brooms made from bundled willow twigs. Even inside, a sprig of mint is set on the table beside a simple meal, a token that the cycle of growth and renewal is underway.

Rain begins to fall—just a sudden, playful shower, the kind that darkens the roofs and brings the smell of crushed herbs from the earth. The traveler, undeterred, laughs and turns his face skyward, feeling the gentle drops on his skin. In this moment, everything feels possible: the path ahead is washed clean, the old fears rinsed away, and the air shimmers with quiet hope.

Today, someone might gather fresh mint or cress to brighten a salad, or pause by a brook to watch the water rush past the rooted clusters of greens. Brewing a cup of tea from just-picked leaves can mark the start of something new, while tending a small kitchen garden or foraging along a trail can bring a sense of adventure into an ordinary day.
