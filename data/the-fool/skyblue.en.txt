THE FOOL ― Upright

A narrow footpath curves along the edge of a wild cliff, a single rose tucked behind an ear and a little dog at the traveler’s heel. The Fool steps forward, eyes wide with the marvel of beginnings, the air crisp and endless above. The day has just begun, promising the first act of a story, open as an empty journal. Overhead, a lark flashes across vast weather, lending its song to the hush before a fall or a flight. The world is nothing but possibility, whistling in the rush of wind and the hush of sun high above.

When the ancients spoke of journeys, they spoke of the open sky. Merchants once watched the vault overhead for signs of fair weather before setting out across the steppe or the sea, trusting in the dome above to keep storms at bay. In stories, the step from the known to the unknown is always taken under this calm expanse, the same that shepherds measure by the shadows of drifting kites. Centuries ago, travelers rode beneath it, following the silk paths from the Eastern cities into the unknown, their packs full of hope and a loaf of bread. In each tale, the open dome is both map and companion, guiding the way with its boundless invitation.

Farmers and sailors, both, would wait for the gentlest clarity above before sowing seed or raising anchor. It is the clarity seen from mountaintops, after a night of thunder, when everything is washed clean and every cloud scuttles low along the horizon. Pilgrims to sacred peaks have knelt in the grass to fill their hands with dew, looking up and feeling the pulse of promise as morning deepens overhead. Poets invoke it when they speak of wide valleys and endless afternoons, when the air is clear enough to hear the distant bells from a far-off village.

In the kitchen, a porcelain bowl sits on the windowsill, filled with cool water, reflecting the overhead gulf in its trembling surface. The bowl once belonged to a grandmother, who used it for rinsing wild berries gathered at the hedge. On festival days, woven streamers and lanterns are strung across courtyards, echoing the openness above, each one a wish for luck as new ventures begin. Children run barefoot across lawns, chasing paper airplanes that rise and fall in that same gentle space, carrying their laughter upward.

The Fool’s small dog barks at the breeze, catching scents from distant fields. In the pocket of the traveler’s coat, the rose’s petals tremble, holding the promise of gardens not yet seen. The road ahead is bright, the air silent but for the whirring of insects and the steady beat of a hopeful heart, daring to begin.

If the day ahead seems uncertain, stand outside for a moment and watch the high arcs of migrating birds. Pour yourself water in a beloved vessel and drink it slowly, feeling the hush of morning. Write a wish or a plan on a slip of paper and let it fly from your window, trusting the air to carry it onward. Step lightly, with the trust of a wanderer at the world’s edge.
