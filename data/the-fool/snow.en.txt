THE FOOL ― Upright

The Fool stands at the very edge, a rose in hand, a small dog at his heel, the journey still unwritten. His step is light, almost careless, chasing after what lies beyond the horizon. Yet all around him there is the hush of possibility, the world made still in anticipation. The cliff drops away, and what waits below is unknown, but above all is silent and bright, as if the land itself has put on new clothes for the first day.

In ancient stories, the first steps of a wanderer sometimes come as the world is transformed by a quiet storm. Imagine the morning after the sky has sifted down a cover that makes every tree, rooftop, and field new. Branches are capped and fences softened, the ground gently lifted in clean drifts. The air seems freshly laundered, sounds muffled and footfalls crisp. Travelers through tundra or mountain passes have measured distance not by markers, but by how many footprints are swallowed up in the quiet aftermath. In Japan, poets watch the hush gather on temple roofs and plum branches, writing verses about beginnings, purity, and the blank page. In the Alps, the first passersby carve pathways that vanish almost as soon as they are made, each step an act of faith.

Within homes, a simple linen laid across a table sets the stage for something new. The cloth, still unmarked, invites bread and conversation, the start to a gathering or a meal. Some cover themselves with woven cotton pulled fresh from a drying line, threads soft against skin after a cold bath, the sense of renewal immediate. The Fool, in his innocence, might find the world has been swept clear for him alone, the traces of those who came before hidden under a soft mantle.

Beyond household rituals, the rose he carries is as untouched as the morning frost, petals unbruised by yesterday's wind. In gardens across the world, petals sometimes catch a thin veil of frozen dew at dawn, catching the light but melting at a touch—a fleeting invitation to begin again. The small dog at his side bounces ahead, nose buried in the untouched ground, trailing a line of pawprints that disappear behind. Their journey is marked by what is unspoiled, landscapes that offer no hints, only the promise that each step will write something new.

The first snowdrops of late winter push through the softened earth, their bell-shaped blossoms nodding beneath the weight of lingering frost. Botanists and children alike bend close to greet these flowers, seeing in them the boldness of the first venture into uncertainty. In older times, the appearance of these blossoms meant the end of waiting, the first answer after silence.

Today, you might open your window to let in the hush of a chilly morning, feeling the world pause and reset. Fold a fresh napkin or towel, smoothing it with your hand before setting the table for breakfast. Take a slow walk outside after a storm, leaving soft prints in the fresh cover, and watch how they blur with each new step. Brew a pot of rice, letting steam rise in gentle clouds, and think of beginnings unmarked by any story but your own.
