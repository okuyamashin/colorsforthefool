THE HIEROPHANT ― Upright

A pair of keys lies on the altar, their weight signifying ancient trust. The Hierophant stands robed in ceremonial garb, the fabric traced with the memory of long, deliberate labor. Pillars of stone enclose the chamber, a temple raised by hands repeating an age-old blueprint. Two attendants kneel in silence, their gaze steady on the ritual as it unfolds, every gesture echoing a pattern preserved across generations. This night’s procedure is shaped by what has always been done—each step a thread in an endless tapestry of tradition.

Beyond the temple, the fields stretch toward the horizon, each stalk of flax swaying gently in the evening breeze. At harvest, hands reach down, tugging the plants from earth made rich by centuries of the same careful tending. Stems are bundled and set to dry in the mild light, and the process of transforming plant to fiber begins. In outbuildings, the work continues: soaking, separating, combing. Simple wooden tools scrape the stalks, coaxing from them filaments fine and strong. Each stage, from breaking to spinning, is an act of devotion to what was learned from elders and neighbors, a knowledge passed from tongue to hand.

Inside kitchens and weaving rooms, the spinning wheel hums with quiet music. Fingers twist thread, winding it carefully, then pass it to the loom. The shuttle travels back and forth, building a fabric that will become coverings for tables, robes for ceremony, and shrouds for the hopes of sleep. These finished cloths are folded in chests, set aside for births, unions, and farewells. At the altar, every vestment and sacred cloth, every stitch in the edge of a book or the hem of a child’s garment, echoes the fields and the hours of steady work. The pattern in the weave tells of ancestors who harvested, spun, and stitched for those who would come after.

As dusk falls, the attendants tidy the floor with brooms fashioned from leftover stalks. Candles flicker on the altar, their light softening the lines of familiar tapestries hanging overhead. Outside, wash lines stretch between fenceposts, garments airing in the gentle wind, each one pressed by careful hands. At supper, elders pull up chairs to a table set with a homespun cloth, breaking bread as they share stories that wind through the fabric of their lives.

In the square of the old market town, vendors open strongboxes, revealing folded cloths as precious as coin. Shoppers test the weight, the texture, considering what is needed for a new season or a new household. Bolts are traded for food, for oil, for sturdy tools, and the fabric finds its way into dowries and simple gifts. When rain taps softly on the roof at night, the cool press of bedclothes brings a sense of completion, sheltering the dreams carried in every inherited routine.

Tonight, you might smooth a table covering before a meal, recite a quiet grace, or wrap something cherished with care. Through these gestures, you step into the old rhythm, guided by the keys of habit and the wisdom of what endures.
