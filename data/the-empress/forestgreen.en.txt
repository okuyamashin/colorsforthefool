THE EMPRESS ― Upright

A low stone wall marks the border of the Empress’s garden. The wheat bends and nods in the wind, its soft heads brushing against berry-thick bramble, and the roses pour their scent over ripe patches of earth. You find her seated, not on a throne, but on a bench of moss-laden timber, sun-warm and easy as a hillside. At her feet lies a basket brimming with apples, pears, and just-picked beans; the air trembles with the sound of a waterfall slipping between rocks at the edge of the clearing.

The world of the Empress is shaped by the forest’s quiet generosity. The trunks of ancient trees rise behind her garden, columns rough with lichen and wrapped in the memory of rain. Between the roots, ferns spread like the hands of old healers, and damp clover carpets the ground. Wild garlic grows in gentle swathes, its scent mingling with the sweetness of meadow grass. From these woods, people have carried home bundles of firewood, woven baskets from willow branches, and gathered mushrooms after autumn’s first long rains.

In the old country, the forest was both refuge and resource. When storms threatened the fields, villagers would shelter beneath its boughs, listening to the patter of rain on spruce needles. Children traced the streams that slipped through the undergrowth, hoping to catch a glimpse of a deer or a flash of trout in the shallows. In the hush of the wood, herbalists sought the leaves and bark that cured fevers and soothed swelling, while shepherds guided their flocks along the cool, dappled paths. Even the cloth draped over the Empress’s shoulders, soft as the underleaf of a lime tree, was dyed with what the forest yielded: nettles, walnut husks, or the juice from a patch of stinging sorrel.

Myth lingers in every glade, woven into the rustle of leaves and the trickle of water. Folklore speaks of the forest’s heart as a place where abundance lives, a living wellspring that both feeds and protects. The Empress’s garden gathers that myth and lays it gently at your feet, transforming it from legend into the bread you break and the fruit you taste. The waterfall, always moving, is a reminder that what is plentiful must be cherished, drawn up in cupped hands whenever you thirst. Abundance is here, not as a distant promise, but in the breathing present: in the scent of rosemary lifted by the wind, in the soft cushion of moss, in the fruit resting heavy among the nettles.

Today, seek out the quiet shelter of trees and walk their shaded paths, feeling the brush of leaves against your arm. Cook with herbs you have picked yourself, or brew a simple tea from what your cupboard holds—bay leaf, mint, or a sprig of thyme. Sit on a wooden bench, or upon the grass if you find it, and close your eyes to hear the nearby water, whether it is a city fountain, a kitchen tap, or the memory of rain. Place a sprig of fern or clover on your desk as a sign of welcome and a small invitation to abundance.
