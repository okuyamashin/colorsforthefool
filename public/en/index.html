How to draw

  1. Draw one card. It comes from the twenty-two major arcana, upright or reversed.
  2. Read what that card stands for today.
  3. Tap again. Today's lucky color opens at the end of the story. Tap the mirror beneath it, and the writing folds away. The card turns face down.

About this site

Each major arcana card is tied to a color. The story moves through myth, history, a place, or a tool, and lands on today's lucky color.

The Fool's Journey

The twenty-two major arcana

  • The Fool

    UprightA solitary figure stands at the very edge of a cliff, travel pack light, a white rose in hand, and a loyal dog close by.

    ReversedThe same figure now hesitates at the cliff, unsure if the ground beneath is safe.

  • The Magician

    UprightA table stands ready with a wand, a cup, a sword, and a coin—each a tool for shaping the day.

    ReversedThe tools on the table are arranged only for display, not for use.

  • The High Priestess

    UprightA woman sits quietly between two pillars, her scroll only half-unfolded on her lap.

    ReversedThe scroll is either unopened, its secrets withheld, or thrown wide, its contents scattered and misunderstood.

  • The Empress

    UprightThe Empress is a place of abundance, where grain ripens, forests shelter, and clear water flows.

    ReversedThe ground may be tired, or you may be rushing to harvest before the fruit is truly ready.

  • The Emperor

    UprightThe Emperor sits on a throne of stone, his gaze fixed far beyond the present.

    ReversedThe throne no longer fits the Emperor.

  • The Hierophant

    UprightA figure sits before two seekers, embodying the transfer of knowledge through tradition and established forms.

    ReversedThe forms have become heavier than their purpose; ritual outweighs meaning.

  • The Lovers

    UprightThe Lovers card represents the moment where two distinct forces meet, asking you to make a conscious and heartfelt choice.

    ReversedThe Lovers reversed points to indecision and the discomfort of leaving choices unfinished.

  • The Chariot

    UprightA charioteer grips the reins, guiding a vehicle drawn by two distinct creatures.

    ReversedThe chariot is in motion, but it has not crossed the threshold.

  • Strength

    UprightStrength is not about how tightly you hold or how much you can suppress.

    ReversedWhen reversed, Strength reveals a hand gone rigid, fingers clenched not out of mastery but out of fear.

  • The Hermit

    UprightThe Hermit stands alone on a quiet mountain, lantern raised just enough to show his next steps.

    ReversedThe Hermit’s lantern now shines only inward, failing to light the path ahead.

  • Wheel of Fortune

    UprightThe Wheel of Fortune turns, exchanging positions between those who ascend and those who descend.

    ReversedIt can seem as though the wheel is spinning in place, giving the illusion of movement without progress.

  • Justice

    UprightJustice is the meeting point of decision and discernment, guided by both the sword and the scales.

    ReversedReversed, Justice warns of imbalance or self-justification.

  • The Hanged Man

    UprightA man hangs upside-down, one foot bound and his arms open, embodying stillness amidst discomfort.

    ReversedHanging upside down has lost its purpose, turning from a search for insight into a habit of delay.

  • Death

    UprightDeath arrives as the silent harbinger of completion.

    ReversedDespite the approach of an ending, effort is spent maintaining the familiar shape.

  • Temperance

    UprightTemperance invites you to blend opposing elements with care, like water flowing gently between two cups.

    ReversedTemperance reversed shows water spilling outside the cups, signaling imbalance or a failed attempt to combine.

  • The Devil

    UprightTwo figures stand chained, the links thick and heavy at first glance, yet the collars around their necks are loose enough to remove.

    ReversedThe chains, once hidden, come into view.

  • The Tower

    UprightA tall structure, once steadfast, is struck by sudden force, exposing its vulnerabilities.

    ReversedAfter the lightning, there is a temptation to rebuild the tower exactly as it was, restoring every stone to its previous, precarious height.

  • The Star

    UprightBy calm waters, a figure kneels and pours water from two jars—one into the earth, one into the stream.

    ReversedThe jars tilt, and water spills into just one spot, stagnating rather than flowing.

  • The Moon

    UprightUnder the uncertain glow of the moon, only part of the path is visible between two silent towers.

    ReversedThe path disappears beneath fog, or else every shadow feels threatening, shaped by anxious imagination.

  • The Sun

    UprightThe Sun signals a time when light gently illuminates what was hidden, allowing things to be seen without shame or confusion.

    ReversedThe reversed Sun warns of too much brightness, where even the shadows are forced into view and nothing can rest.

  • Judgement

    UprightA clear call echoes, stirring figures from their long rest.

    ReversedThe trumpet sounds, but you press down the lid from inside your own enclosure.

  • The World

    UprightA figure dances gracefully within a ring, watched over from all corners.

    ReversedThe dance pauses just before the ring is closed.

Questions

How do upright and reversed differ?

The same picture changes its sign when it turns. Upright is the card opened. Reversed is delay, excess, or the meaning turned over. A reversed picture is the card rotated halfway around.

Can I draw every day?

One card a day, or several in a row. When the deck is empty, it is whole again.
