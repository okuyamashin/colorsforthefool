Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / TEMPERANCE

MediumAquaMarine

Upright · #66CDAA

On the Temperance card, a robed figure stands poised between flowing water and firm earth. One cup pours into another, a measured movement that avoids haste or waste. The pool below mirrors both the sky and the steady hands above it, a stage for the patient passing of water between vessels. Beyond the pool, a wandering path winds up a gentle ridge, signaling movement and quiet progress, the distance between start and finish softened by the landscape’s calm.

Centuries ago, sailors prized a certain stone found along the shorelines where rivers mingle with the sea. It was said that these stones carried the memory of safe harbors—smooth tides, steady breezes, and the rare, translucent clarity of seawater glimpsed at dawn. Sailors would sew such a stone into the lining of their jackets, trusting it to keep tempers cool and navigation true. When disputes burst out below deck, the captain might press one into a palm, a reminder that even the most turbulent waters can settle if given time.

Further inland, artisans and merchants learned to echo this same serenity in their craft. Glassblowers, breathing life into molten sand, coaxed bowls and vases to hold the quiet gleam of a tidal pool. These vessels were not just beautiful but practical. On a kitchen table, one might cradle the first peas of spring, their pods crisp from the garden, or a handful of mint meant for an evening’s tea. Each time water was poured into such a bowl, the act carried a silent invocation: careful, attentive, and grateful for whatever was given into one’s care.

In the world of plants, a similar presence appears. On mild coastlines and high, stony ridges, there are succulents whose leaves collect and hold rain, storing coolness against the heat of midday. Gardeners learn to respect these survivors, watering them sparingly, letting the leaves teach lessons in moderation. Not too much, never too little—just enough. Sometimes, after a summer shower, a hummingbird hovers to sip from a leaf's hollow, then darts away, leaving the plant undisturbed, equilibrium restored.

Weather, too, practices the art of mixing. On certain mornings, just after a gentle rain, the world is wrapped in a soft, clean mist. It blurs the sharp edges and draws out the subtle lines of path and garden. This is the moment to step outside and breathe the air as if it has been freshly strained through linen. The day begins without rush, each footfall deliberate, each breath part of the quiet work of reconciling past and future—like water passing from cup to cup, leaving nothing behind.

Today, you might carry a sea-polished stone in your pocket, letting its weight steady your thoughts. Pour fresh water into a glass bowl and pause to watch how sunlight plays on its surface, a moment of reflection before the day’s tasks. Attend to a drought-loving plant, watering only as much as it truly needs, and trust in its slow, silent growth. Or, after the rain, walk without aim, letting the mist guide your pace, your path shaped by patience.
