Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / TEMPERANCE

Gold

Upright · #FFD700

The image is quiet and deliberate: water pours gently from one vessel into another, hands steady and patient. Beneath, a pool lies still, reflecting the passage above. Beyond, a narrow path ascends a gentle ridge, a promise of perspective after careful blending. The work here is measured and clear—no rushing, no waste. Where water finds balance, something subtle yet momentous is being accomplished: the mingling of two things, never perfectly alike, into a new coherence.

Throughout the old empires, there was a metal people held above all others. Kings and priests hammered it into bands for their arms, crowns for their heads, coins for their vaults. Rivers in distant mountains hid flecks of it among stones, and prospectors sifted sand for the rare shimmer that sparkled in sunlight. It became the stuff of longing, journeys, and treaties, a mark of civilization's reach. In Greek myth, heroes chase after apples of this metal in far-off gardens, guarded by dragons. Midas, with a careless touch, turned his table and his food into the same rare thing, learning caution the hard way. In Egypt, its leaf adorned the tombs of pharaohs, crossing the boundary between life and death as a promise against decay.

In the home, it shows up in tools of both celebration and restraint. There are rings passed between lovers, a signal of vows made and kept. Small coins rest in old purses, saved for when times are lean. At New Year’s, some households stir a hard nugget, wrapped in cloth, into a loaf of bread—a quiet wish for prosperity in the year ahead. In kitchens, a fine mesh strainer, plated with this metal, is used to bring out the clearest broth from a simmering pot, the essence of patience and care. On feast days, thread is spun so thin it can be woven into robes or stitched into icons, catching the light as it moves, never overbearing, only hinting at the treasure worked into the fabric.

Weather, too, can echo this card's spirit. There are mornings when the first rays filter over the hills, illuminating dew on wild grasses and the roofs of distant villages. In such moments, the landscape seems poured over with a gentle shine, not harsh, but glimmering with promise. The world is hushed, as if waiting for the work of the day to begin, and the light is both invitation and benediction. In autumn, fields of wheat, ripe before harvest, sway under a high sky, their motion the very image of blending and readiness.

To bring the essence of mixing and patience into the present, a person might set aside a coin, not for spending, but as a reminder of value gained over time. One might stir honey into tea, watching the ribbon dissolve slowly, and drink while thinking of what is being gathered quietly in life. Writing a wish or intention on a slip of paper and placing it under a favorite piece of jewelry can serve as a silent commitment to balance and harmony. Walking early in the morning, when the world is quiet and the light is still new, lets the mind settle into the work of gentle transformation.
