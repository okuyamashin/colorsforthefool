Draw a card / THE EMPRESS

LavenderBlush

女帝 · ラベンダーブラッシュ

Upright · #FFF0F5

The Empress sits in her garden, the throne cushioned and low, encircled by wheat that nods beneath a soft breeze. Her domain is a tangle of fruiting vines and the cool rush of water threading through stone, a patchwork of abundance that asks for nothing and shares everything. Roses climb a lattice nearby, petals open and unhurried, their scent traveling with the light air. She gathers her dress and steps from the seat, to where the wheat gives way to beds of flowering herbs and stems that sway beneath the sky.

From the garden’s edge, she remembers a time when her forerunners sowed more than grain. On Mediterranean hills, rows of lavender flourished, their blooms gathered at dusk by careful hands. Homesteaders distilled their essence at hearthside, steam rising in the kitchen, the scent curling into the rafters. Bundles dried over the doors, and sachets hid in linen chests, keeping cloth sweet through the seasons. In France, entire families once harvested spikes along the high plateaus, turning meadows into living seas, hands and baskets fragrant and busy. The fields, when the wind shifted, carried a haze that seemed to settle on everything—a sign to pause, to breathe, to steep the world in calm.

Meanwhile, generations elsewhere muddled flower syrup for cakes, or folded it into warm milk before sleep. In English gardens, a border of these blooms marked the threshold between wildness and order, the same way the Empress’s seat marks the center of her world. On stormy days, women stirred blossoms into hot water, letting steam soothe the day's ache. On quiet afternoons, a mother would brush the dust from old porcelain, painted long ago with sprays of this same flowering stalk—tea poured, sugared biscuits set out, a table heaped with small, good things. The color remained on china, in recipes, in the soft fabric of church dresses worn at mid-summer, and in the gentle print lining a child’s cradle.

The Empress breathes the humid air. Nearby, the stones glisten from a recent shower, and the waterfall brims just a little higher, its voice a steady hush. It is the kind of weather that invites growth, softness, and patience; the sky holds that after-rain hush, and the light through the lingering mist brings a kind of clarity. In this garden, abundance is neither loud nor urgent. Fruit ripens by the hour, petals open to the dew, and the gentle world softens its edges. The Empress lifts a stalk, fingers brushing the buds, and listens to the water’s song.

Today, you might dry a handful of flowers to slip beneath your pillow, or brew a mug of something floral and warm as evening settles. You could find a scrap of cloth stitched with old patterns, or walk where the rain has just washed the air clean, letting your thoughts open. Abundance, she suggests, is often already close at hand—a gentle scent, a moment’s calm, a chair beside a window.
