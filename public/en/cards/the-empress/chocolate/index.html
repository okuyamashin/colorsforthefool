Chocolate

女帝 · チョコレート

Reversed · #D2691E

The garden is quiet now, its paths overrun by last year’s weeds. The gate leans on its hinge, and the stone steps are masked by fallen leaves. Once, the Empress watched over these rows of beans and vines, her arms filled with baskets of late summer pods and ripe pods split open by careful hands. Birds sang in the mulberry trees, and the air smelled of warm soil. Now, with the Empress turned away, the land grows wild, and the fruit does not find its place at the table.

In the heart of this garden, a sturdy tree once offered shade, its pods hanging heavy. In distant lands, this tree rises above the undergrowth, its broad leaves sheltering the ground from harsh sun. The pods, rough and ridged, conceal within their hard shells a treasure sought by travelers, traders, and poets alike. In monsoon weather, the roots drink deep, mud clinging to bare feet as workers gather the harvest. Far from here, in villages pressed between rivers and jungle, these pods are split and laid out on mats to dry beneath the uncertain sky.

Long ago, these seeds made their way by stream and ship, packed into crates bound for distant markets. They passed through the hands of sailors and merchants, finding their way into kitchens where spiced drinks steamed in clay mugs. In noble courts, the fragrance of roasted beans drifted through tiled halls, accompanied by the sound of pestle grinding against mortar. Over time, the seeds were pressed and mixed, poured into wooden molds, wrapped in paper by nimble fingers. They became tokens exchanged at holidays, and small luxuries folded into a shawl or hidden away in a desk drawer.

In the garden’s quiet neglect, the absence of tending is felt. The baskets are empty, the earth unturned. The tools—spade, knife, and trowel—rest beneath a thin film of dust. The heavy comfort of the garden’s shelter now presses down, a reminder that abundance must be coaxed, and gifts do not gather themselves. The harvest, left ungathered, slips away: pods rot in the damp, seeds are carried off by ants, and the pulse of the seasons grows faint.

Yet from these same seeds, there is a lesson. Even when harvests are lost and the kitchen silent, the memory of warmth remains. A piece of fruit, dried and bitter, can be ground and sweetened, transformed by patient hands into comfort for a weary spirit. The alchemy of the kitchen echoes the transformation once coaxed from the living earth. The vessels that held drink in ancient courts and the bars wrapped in foil in household pantries all trace their lineage to these neglected pods.

Today, you may step into the forgotten garden and gather any fallen pod, if only in memory. You may warm your hands around a cup of spiced drink, noticing the aroma that rises. Write a letter to someone far away, enclosing a small morsel as a gesture of peace. Sweep the dust from your kitchen tools and leave them ready for another season.
