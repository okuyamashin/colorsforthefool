Draw a card / THE EMPRESS

PeachPuff

女帝 · ピーチパフ

Upright · #FFDAB9

In the shade of the orchard, the Empress sits where the wild grasses yield to neat beds of wheat and trailing vines. Clusters of roses wind through the low hedges, their petals falling onto the stone path. The seat beneath her is carved from pear wood, worn smooth by seasons, and set beside a steady waterfall that pours over moss-covered rocks. This is not a place of want, but of fulfillment already realized: baskets of ripe fruit rest by her feet, spilling with peaches so soft their fuzz catches the morning dew, and loaves of bread still warm from the oven lie next to them, their crusts cracking open with promise. The air is thick with the scents of baking and ripening, a mingling of orchard and hearth.

In the heart of this garden, abundance is a quiet chorus. The peach trees, heavy with their blushing harvest, have stood here for generations. Their trunks are painted with the dust of summers, and their roots drink deeply from the nearby stream. When the sun is soft and low, the kitchen windows glow with the steam curling from preserves: jars lined along the shelves, each holding the memory of July afternoons. Historian’s tales tell of traders in distant markets who wrapped peaches in linen to carry their sweetness across mountains, and of old country kitchens where their flesh was folded into pastry dough, baked until clouds of flaky crust rose to greet hungry hands.

The light in this scene is gentle, filtered through fine curtains sewn from the fabric that once clothed children in summer, a fabric spun to be sheer and soft on skin, capturing the color of the fruit at its most fragrant. These curtains move with the breeze, letting in the garden’s hush and the distant hum of bees, while keeping the gentle flavors of midday sunlight alive within the house. The waterfall at the garden’s edge is a blessing, its spray cooling the leaves and washing stone bowls left out for birds. In the garden’s weather, nothing is rushed—mornings unfold as a warm hush, as if the sky itself were swaddled in a pale, yielding veil.

Abundance, here, is not loud. It reveals itself in simple gestures: a slice of peach placed atop a piece of buttered toast, a bowl of custard whipped until airy and topped with fruit, a soft cloth laid on a table beneath a dish of summer preserves. The Empress gathers these things not for show, but because tending them is the daily art of living well. She invites others to join, to taste and to take comfort in the labor that brings forth sweetness and rest.

Today, a person might simmer peaches with honey and spoon them over breakfast, or open a window so the scent of baking bread drifts into the afternoon. Someone could fold a fresh cloth and place it beneath a vase of cut roses, or walk beneath the peach trees and let a soft breeze carry the orchard’s hush into their hands.
