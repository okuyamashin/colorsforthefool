DimGray

愚者 · ディムグレイ

Reversed · #696969

At the edge of the path, the young wanderer lifts his boot, not knowing where it will fall. In tales and painted cards, he trusts in sunlit stones and the dog’s cheer, but now, an overcast morning cloaks the land. The sky is quilted with low, rain-brewing clouds, and the air carries the hush of something left unexamined. His knapsack is patched and worn, the fabric showing the marks of many careless rests on soot-dusted steps and the cold bones of last season’s fires. In his hand, a walking stick, once polished by his enthusiasm, now dulled from unplanned scrapes against iron bridges and crumbling train tracks.

He skips along a lane where the world is outlined by the subtle presence of what endures after brightness slips away. Buildings rise on either side, their stone frontages streaked by decades of city smoke and the patient touch of fog. Doorways are flanked by planters where the lavender is nearly spent, and the only plant thriving is the old lichen, brushing the surface of forgotten benches and the rims of gutters. The Fool’s laughter, always bright, is hesitant now, muffled by the kind of weather that signals a pause—drizzle that never becomes rain, a sky that neither clears nor tires.

In the old quarter, he passes the blacksmith’s shop, shuttered for the day. The anvil at the threshold is cold and silent, its surface holding stories of tools shaped in the glow of forge and ambition. The cobblestones, slick from yesterday’s mist, echo beneath his feet with a soft percussion. Still, he looks up, drawn to the narrow chimney stacks and slate roofs, each layered with the same city residue, each surviving winter after winter in the hush of endurance. There is a bakery around the corner, its windows fogged by low fires. The baker’s bread rests on trays of hammered tin, crusted and hearty, dusted from flour and the settling of ash as the ovens cool at dusk.

He feels the pause in the world, as if the next step should be measured, the ground tested before leaping forward. In the garden behind a boarding house, a tangle of willowherb grows next to a pile of stone pavers, the plants weaving through gaps where mortar has failed. Potatoes rest in their burlap sack, set aside for dinner, still marked by soil clinging from the last harvest. Above, a pigeon perches on the wire, feathers ruffled against a chill draft, waiting for some unseen signal to move.

From these things, the wanderer learns quiet caution. He can draw a circle on the hearthstone with the tip of his boot and promise himself to pause before he leaps. He might soak his hands in the tin basin at day’s end, letting the water cool to match the air, reminding himself that not every moment calls for haste. He could gather the spent lavender from the planters and set it beside his bed, a mild fragrance to weigh him toward sleep before any journey. He might sit on the steps as the mist lifts, tracing the old lines of soot with his finger, reminding himself to study the ground before he dances forward.
