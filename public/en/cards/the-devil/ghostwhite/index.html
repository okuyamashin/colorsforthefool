GhostWhite

悪魔 · ゴーストホワイト

Reversed · #F8F8FF

At the center of the card, two figures sit, their shackles loose about their ankles. They seem unaware of the room’s silence, or perhaps they savor it, continuing to linger in their places, as if the act of wearing the chain is now more habit than bondage. The floor beneath them is the ancient stone of a crypt, smooth and cold beneath bare feet, swept clean but never fully rid of dust. The air stirs as if someone has just crossed through, though no one is visible. Only a faint imprint of breath lingers where presence once was. There is no threat now; the horned shadow on the wall has faded, and with it much of the dramatic struggle. What remains is the quiet, almost antiseptic emptiness that follows release—a stillness that can be mistaken for peace, or for denial.

In this space, the mind turns to the things that mark absence as much as presence: old bone china set on a table after midnight, its glaze thin as a whisper. The untouched porcelain bowl at the end of the hallway, placed to catch a soft drip from the ceiling, is a sentinel to the old house’s secret weather. Along the baseboard grows dust, gathering like frost on a windowpane, a gentle veil that muffles sound and time. On the dresser, a pressed handkerchief, starched and folded, waits for a hand that may or may not reach for it. These are the things of hospital rooms long emptied, of guest rooms prepared for a visitor who never arrives. Even the garden, at dawn, shows the same hush: the night-blooming jasmine clings with pale fingers to the trellis, heady and unseen, as the fog rolls in from the river beyond the hedge.

It is said, in mountain villages, that mist is the breath of the ancestors, rising from the soil at daybreak. Shepherds who once wandered beneath the limestone cliffs would listen for the bells of sheep moving unseen in the shroud, and step quietly, knowing the veil was thin between what is forgotten and what remains. In some coastal towns, the fishermen mend their nets by lantern in the predawn hours, surrounded by the hush of everything not yet revealed, their hands working almost by memory. In old cathedrals, the marble effigies of saints and angels gather dust, their faces softened by centuries, their open arms a gesture no longer sharp with warning but gentle, as if beckoning the living to rest.

Weathered gravestones stand in the churchyard, names and years nearly effaced, but each stone is carefully tended. Moss is brushed away, and lilies are left, pale and fragrant, as offerings. The work is methodical, reverent, and calm. In far northern lands, after a night of heavy snow, children walk out at sunrise to find the world erased and ready for reimagining. Their footprints are the first marks in the new day, quiet as secrets.

If you wish to honor this day, you might lay out the best tablecloth, even if no guests are expected, or open a window just before dawn and let the still air touch your face. Fold your favorite shirt with care, smoothing every invisible crease, or write a letter to someone who exists mostly in your memories and place it in a drawer, sealed and safe.
