Silver

悪魔 · シルバー

Reversed · #C0C0C0

Two figures in the shadow of the archway, their wrists circled by chains as loose as a forgotten scarf. The stone floor is damp, echoing the world above. Something holds them, but the hold is choice, not sentence. Above, the horned one waits, but this time with a gaze less piercing, more patient, as if expecting them to rise of their own accord.

In old stories, a mirror is a threshold to the underworld. The surface of the mirror, flat and cold, has witnessed confessions long withheld. It is a plate from which the moon eats, a piece of the night sky framed for the vain or the penitent. In the house, someone wipes the surface clean, seeing a face both familiar and otherwise, the chain at the throat now an ornament, not a shackle. The reflection is more honest than the mind’s memory.

Far north, in the months when light fades quickly, the river freezes. Under the crust, the current is still alive, unseen. Fishermen bore holes through the frozen skin, listening to the echo ring through metal buckets. The chain on a trap snaps free with one practiced twist. This river does not demand, but it watches; its surface gleams, hiding the depth below, and the ice does not care if feet tarry or stride. The villagers string flatware and bells above their doors, remnants of old spoons and serving forks, wards against what they fear most: not the devil, but silence and stagnation.

A legend from the city of trade: merchants once carried coins pressed with the faces of vanished kings, stamped anew for each crossing of the mountains. In their pouches, ancient disks mingled with the fresh, memory and ambition together. They exchanged these for salt, for bread, for letters of passage. In the night, they poured their wealth onto the table, letting the disk of each coin ring out, a sound measured and cold, an assurance. The fortune could be taken or left, but the sound itself lingered, promising freedom by daybreak.

In kitchens, the drawer of utensils holds an old knife, its blade dulled from years of paring apples and slicing bread. The handle is worn smooth. It is no longer sharp enough for harm, yet is kept for comfort, for memory. Nearby, a teapot with a dented lid, reflecting lamplight, shares the shelf with a bowl used for tossing coins to decide small matters. Someone picks up the cup, watches their hand tremble in the curved reflection, and sets it down. The chain stays on, but so loosely it almost falls away with each movement.

Outside, clouds gather at dusk. They do not threaten storm, but settle gently, wrapped in a hush that covers rooftops and bare branches. The air is electric, as if the ground waits for a signal. Somewhere a breeze flicks at chimes made of old teaspoons, singing a tune that is not quite mournful. The weather is neither fierce nor calm, but holds its breath as if waiting for some decision to be made. The shadow at the threshold is not banished, but observed from the comfort of one's own door.

Today, anyone can clear the bathroom mirror and see what truths remain unspoken, or walk along the riverbank and listen for what moves beneath the frozen crust. One can clean a cherished knife and recall a time it served more than memory, or ring a handful of saved coins on the tabletop, letting their music settle questions left unasked.

Today's lucky color is Silver.
