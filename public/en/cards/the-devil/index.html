THE DEVIL

悪魔 · XV

Upright

Two figures stand chained, the links thick and heavy at first glance, yet the collars around their necks are loose enough to remove. The Devil presides, not as an external captor, but as the echo of a belief: that the chains cannot be escaped, when in fact, they are voluntary. It is a state where the illusion of being trapped feels just as real as any prison.

Desires themselves are not the cage. The true confinement comes from refusing to acknowledge them, letting nameless cravings define identity and habit. The tools of daily life—approval, routine, laughter, material comfort—can just as easily become shackles when left unexamined.

This card today invites a quiet test: slip your fingers beneath the collar, and see if it is truly tight. It is a moment to question assumptions, to realize that what binds you may not be as inescapable as it seems.

Reversed

The chains, once hidden, come into view. Perhaps the collar has already been removed, yet its shape lingers, an invisible outline still felt at the throat. In this moment, confusion follows the first rush of awareness—a sudden, overwhelming freedom makes the old cage oddly comforting.

When The Devil appears reversed, the process of liberation stalls midway. Insight is gained, but the mind quickly spins a new narrative of guilt or self-reproach. The chains do not disappear; they simply change form, persistence disguised in new habits or regrets.

Today, this card suggests not to pick up the old collar again, nor to mourn the loss of familiar limits. Instead, take just one step outside, however tentative, and begin to live in the unfamiliar space beyond the cage.
