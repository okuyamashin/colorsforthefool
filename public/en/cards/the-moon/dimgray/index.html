Draw a card / THE MOON

DimGray

月 · ディムグレイ

Reversed · #696969

When the moon is veiled, the world along the riverbank loses its outlines. Here, the old card's dog and wolf, once clear in their stations, now move as a single shape through the fog, their steps quiet over the packed earth of the footpath. The reeds bow in the wind, and the surface of the water, usually dappled with reflection, is dull and unreadable. Night deepens, and the path narrows, bordered not by certainty but by the low silhouettes of stone.

Ancient roads across the moors, laid centuries ago by hands unknown, bear the tracks of many travelers. In shifting weather, the ground becomes sodden; boots leave their mark but the mist creeps in, blurring even fresh impressions. Shepherds moving their flocks pause at the edge of visibility, searching for the shapes of sheep among the stones. In towns, roofs of slate guard houses against the drizzle; the tiles wet from steady rain, their surface catching and holding the memory of storms. The air smells of wet wool, hearth smoke, and the start of rot that comes with the close damp.

In the stories told by the fireside, men remember the mines—shafts cut into the hillside, where the daylight is always left behind. The tunnel walls, braced with wood and iron, echo with the tap of hammers and the soft fall of dust. Down in the gloom, time is measured by the click of a tin lunch pail, the weight of a workman’s coat, the kerchief looped at the wrist. Each object serves as both burden and shield, worn and trusted in the darkness where vision is a rumor. When the shift ends, miners step into the open air, blinking, the world above a silent sea under clouds.

On the coast, weathered cobblestones line the harbor, slick with brine after rain. Fishermen mend nets on overturned buckets, hands stained and knuckles scraped, talking in low voices about the tides. Warehouses beside the docks are shuttered, their doors heavy with the salt of many winters. Inside, barrels of preserved fish and crates of tools are stacked in neat, unyielding order, each bearing the marks of use and time. In the corners, a coil of rope waits, smelling of hemp and patience.

Inside kitchens at dawn, the cast-iron kettle is set atop the stove, and the first cup of tea is poured as the window shows only a shape against the light. Hearthstones worn smooth by generations of boots and slippers hold the morning’s warmth. The woolen blanket, thrown over the chair, is mottled from years of mending, a memory of hands that patched and washed it through the seasons. At the table, bread is sliced, crumbs brushed onto a plate that has seen many such mornings.

As the mist hangs low, a person may walk the old road, pausing to trace the lettering on a lichen-covered milestone. You might light a lantern and set it in the window, or wrap yourself in a coat that has weathered many evenings like this. Write a letter by the glow of an iron lamp, or press your palm to the cool stone of the garden wall as you step into the quiet. Each small gesture calls back the steadiness of things built to endure the night.
