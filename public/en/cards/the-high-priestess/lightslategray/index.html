Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / THE HIGH PRIESTESS

LightSlateGray

Reversed · #778899

The High Priestess sits behind her curtain, holding her scroll, but today the curtain is a stone wall and the scroll is unread, turned inwards and silent. In the upright world, her temple is a passage between mysteries, the river behind her a glimmering invitation. Reversed, the river is frozen under clouded skies. The temple’s stones are silent, the air heavy with questions that never find their answers. She becomes a guardian of silence, where knowledge is not shared but sealed behind slate.

Slate itself was once cut from cliffs and mountains, quarried by hands searching for shelter and endurance. Roofers lay these tiles, overlapping each one to shield a home from relentless rain. In Welsh villages, the rooftops catch the mist, their surfaces slick when a soft, persistent drizzle drapes the morning. Old schools, railway stations, and farmhouses carry these same shields, the weight of their age and use thick in the air, as if every tile remembers secrets kept from the wind. Such places do not reveal their heart easily, and the rooms beneath the slates are dim and hushed, the echo of footsteps absorbed as if by fog.

A slate board stands in the corner of a study, its face wiped but never quite clean. Chalk marks, half-remembered, linger in the crevices. The board is an invitation to write, but the stick of chalk is snapped and the lesson unfinished. Students once looked at the board for answers; now they see only the ghost of what might have been written. The mind turns inward, circling questions, unable to break through. This is the knowledge that is withheld ― not lost, but hidden just out of reach.

On a quiet afternoon, rain falls in a slow, unwavering pattern, the kind that seeps into the bones of old stone villages. The sky above is a pale, clouded dome, neither bright nor fully shadowed. Breath fogs against the windowpane as a kettle warms on the range. Outside, mountains loom, their ridges softened by mist and distance. Sheep graze in silence along rocky slopes, their coats thick against the damp. The world feels suspended, held in a hush, as if voices and stories have retreated beneath the roofs and behind the walls, waiting for clearer skies.

In this weather, the High Priestess is the closed gate, the water that holds its secrets. She is the ledger that stays unread, the door that does not yield. Knowledge waits, like the sun behind the clouds, unsure when it will break through. Yet, even in silence, there are gentle actions that can bring comfort to the day. One might sharpen a stick of chalk and quietly write a question, even if no answer comes. Another may smooth the surface of a slate to prepare it for new thoughts, erasing what lingers. A third could stand at the window and watch the wet rooftops, breathing in the hush, letting patience be its own wisdom. Or, with careful hands, a person may patch the missing tile on a roof, mindful that each piece is both shield and memory.
