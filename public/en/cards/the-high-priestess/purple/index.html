Draw a card / THE HIGH PRIESTESS

Purple

女教皇 · パープル

Reversed · #800080

Within the temple’s hush, the High Priestess keeps to her silent post, the long scroll withheld behind folded arms. The veil, which should part to reveal wisdom, now hangs heavy and unmoving. The crescent at her feet is shadowed, the water behind her fixed and mute, offering no reflection. What is hidden is not only unknown but actively locked away, the passage to intuition barred by thick silence. In this airless hush, mysteries do not drift close; they stay stubborn in the dark, refusing to be named.

In such conditions, the world once turned to the rare and precious harvests of distant shores. Along the coastline of Tyre, fishermen gathered shellfish, prying open each creature with practiced hands to find a hidden source of dye. This yielded a pigment that stained fabric with a depth few could possess. Robes treated with this essence became emblems of authority and secrecy, worn by figures whose knowledge was set apart from the crowd. These garments separated rulers from subjects, priests from layfolk; they were more than attire—they were barriers, marking boundaries of what could and could not be known.

Elsewhere, the same word would come to rest on fruit clustered in Mediterranean groves. Trees heavy with globes that hid hundreds of shining seeds became symbols of patience and reward. Breaking one open required effort, and the reward was not immediate; each bite offered only a hint of what the whole fruit contained. The seeds, like guarded thoughts, were scattered but not easily gathered, each one a small promise waiting to be unlocked. On a cold morning, a cook might reach for a jar of preserves made from these fruits, spreading them carefully on bread as a quiet act of comfort, the flavor at once sharp and sweet, recalling the slow, secret labor of harvest.

In the evening, as weather shifts and steady rain falls outside, curtains pulled thick across windows keep the room close and separate from the storm. Books once covered in rare-dyed cloth sit unopened on the shelf, their spines bearing silent witness to knowledge not yet confronted. In monasteries of the past, these books would have rested beneath veils, guarded from light, awaiting the hand and eye permitted to read them. Even now, a ribbon from such cloth might mark a page, its purpose not to reveal, but to keep a place until the time for understanding arrives.

Today, you might brew tea from dried flower petals, letting the steam rise as you sit with something unsolved. You could wrap yourself in a shawl passed down through your family, its fibers woven with care and carrying a weight beyond its warmth. You might split open a fruit, letting the seeds spill into a bowl, savoring each one with attention and leaving the rest for another day. If the rain persists, you could draw the curtains and light a candle, creating a wall between yourself and the noise, allowing space for what cannot be answered yet.
