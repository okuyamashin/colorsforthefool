Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / THE HIGH PRIESTESS

Silver

Upright · #C0C0C0

In the quiet sanctuary of the High Priestess, two pillars stand apart, holding the hush between them. A veil hangs, threads woven tight, separating what is seen from what is only sensed. In her lap, the closed scroll is silent, the secret script waiting for the right moment. At her feet, still water reflects the moon above, a mirror of what lies beneath the surface. This card is the promise of knowing hidden within not-knowing, of wisdom enclosed in a chamber behind the veil.

History, too, has its veils. Along the banks of Anatolia’s rivers, traders once carried bars and coins marked with the mark of Artemis, goddess of the crescent. These were not simple tokens, but markers of trust and protection, valued for the quiet gleam that could not be disguised. Later, in the high vaults of medieval monasteries, monks stored their most precious manuscripts in chests fitted with locks and hinges made by the town’s careful smiths, who worked late by the glow of candlelight. Such chests stood at the heart of libraries, holding texts that whispered of healing herbs and forgotten prayers, secrets bound and guarded, awaiting the patient reader.

Geography lends its own hush. On rare mornings after a winter storm, the land will sleep beneath a sheet of frost, each branch and rooftop outlined in cold, clean lace. Early travelers know the crunch of the path underfoot, the way breath fogs and vanishes. Sometimes, the sky itself lowers, and a fog settles over the fields, muffling all sound, allowing only the cry of a distant bird. In the far north, rivers run with fish whose scales shimmer as they dart among the stones, their movements revealing and concealing in the same instant. In the ancient Andes, a trail of hammered tokens once marked the way for travelers, guiding them in silence across the high passes.

Inside the home, memory lingers in objects passed from hand to hand. The slender spoon in the kitchen drawer, once a christening gift; the lockbox beneath the bed, where a grandmother kept her letters tied with ribbon; a set of candlesticks brought out for nights of reflection or prayer. These objects are quiet guests, offering continuity between generations. On cold evenings, the teapot brought to the table shines beneath the lamplight, steam curling upward—that hush before words are spoken. The hidden drawer in an old desk may hide a brooch, a coin, or a folded note, its story waiting for the right finder.

In stories told by the fire, the High Priestess steps softly. She carries a bowl of quicksilver, mirrored and elusive, guiding dreamers to the edge of sleep. Or she walks the shore at dawn, searching for a strand of ore left by the retreating tide, her footsteps pressing patterns into the sand. She knows the value of patience, of keeping counsel, of letting the answer remain behind the curtain until the moment is ripe.

Today, you might find something hidden in your own daily surroundings—a forgotten keepsake resting at the back of a drawer, or an old coin at the bottom of a jar. You might pause to polish the family candlesticks or run your fingers along a handwritten letter. Brew your tea in the old pot, letting the steam rise in the quiet morning, or step outside at dawn and listen to the silence settling on the earth, a promise kept beneath the surface.
