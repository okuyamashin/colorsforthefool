Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / STRENGTH

Gold

Upright · #FFD700

A quiet woman kneels in the grass, her head circled by a crown of daisies and marigolds. The petals are warm from the sun, soft against her hair. Her hand rests upon a lion’s muzzle, fingers gentle as she eases its jaws closed. No shout, no whip, no threat breaks the stillness; her calm patience flows between them, stronger than any chain. In the hush, the lion’s tail flicks in the dust, muscles easing under her peaceful gaze. The garland she wears is not for conquest, but for harmony—a sign of those who master themselves before commanding the wild.

Long ago, travelers crossed high mountains and forded cold rivers, driven by tales of treasure glimmering beneath the surface. They sifted riverbeds with wooden pans, swirling sand and water until only the heaviest flakes remained—each one a shard of sunlight held in stone. This was not a pursuit for the impatient. The process took hours, days, sometimes years. The river whispered its secrets only to those who returned again and again to its banks, hands steady, hearts quiet, eyes searching for the glint among the pebbles. The reward, small and bright, rested in their palms as proof that gentle persistence can win what force cannot.

In ancient halls, artisans worked with fire and hammers, stretching the precious metal into thin leaves. They pressed these over wooden icons, bows on lyres, and the handles of ceremonial cups, bringing radiance to objects of worship and daily life. The work was painstaking: each leaf smoothed carefully with burnishers, every crease attending to the craftsman’s silent rhythm. In some monasteries, monks used those leaves to illuminate ancient books, their quiet patience turning scripture into lasting treasure. To mark victory, rulers placed gleaming wreaths on the heads of athletes and poets, symbols of glory granted not through brute strength, but through endurance and inner discipline.

In kitchens, honey—dripped from the comb after a patient season—glazes loaves set to cool on wooden boards. Its sweetness, gathered from wildflowers by careful bees, is a quiet celebration at breakfast tables. The old wedding bands, handed down through families, shine with the light of stories and promises kept, their circles a testament to lasting devotion. At the hearth, candlesticks catch the last glow of afternoon, drawing in every eye as the house settles into evening. Outside, wheat ripens through a long summer, each stalk bending in the breeze, heavy with grain that owes its richness to time, not force.

In today’s world, one can honor this tradition of strength by carrying a coin, feeling its promise of reward earned through patience. Drizzle honey on toast, savoring the slow labor of many days in each drop. Polish a brass key, ring, or small tool, letting it gleam as you remember the steady hands that shaped it. Or, twine a flower crown for yourself or a friend, wearing the ancient sign of quiet mastery.
