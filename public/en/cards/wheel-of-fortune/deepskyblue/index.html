Draw a card / WHEEL OF FORTUNE

DeepSkyBlue

運命の輪 · ディープスカイブルー

Upright · #00BFFF

A wheel turns in the clouds, old as myth, the spokes marked by four creatures of learning and fate. Each revolution changes the view: sometimes a lion rises, sometimes the wings of an eagle catch the light, sometimes the book of the angel opens wide, and sometimes the bull stands firm upon the rim. The pattern never stops, always circling, always changing. Beneath the wheel, rivers reflect the sky above, carrying the sun’s path as it moves from cold mornings to warm afternoons, seasons circling just as surely as the turning of fortune.

Travelers once watched the open sky for signs—sailors at sea gauging the day by the color above the horizon, farmers reading the story of tomorrow in the sweep of cloud and clarity. Somewhere along the coast, standing at the rail of a weathered ferry, a person could look up and see the day cast in astonishing clarity overhead. This was the kind of sky that ancient mariners would trust, forging ahead with full hearts, knowing that storms would come in their season, but that today the wind was fair. High above, swallows darted through the upper air, drawing invisible spirals to mark their own tiny wheels.

In high mountain passes, where glaciers have carved old stone, the sky’s openness lifts the spirits of the weary. This is the territory of shepherds and climbers, of poets scribbling notes in threadbare field books, and of traders whose caravans wind along precarious routes. The sky’s vastness becomes a promise: that fortune, just like the weather, may shift at any turn. To look up is to see the span of possibility, the expanse carrying wishes and warnings alike. It is the season when wild gentians bloom between the rocks, blossoms shaped like stars, catching the same clarity that arches overhead.

In the city, laundry lines hung from one building to another flutter in the midday light. Cloths and bedsheets catch the air, drying quickly on days when the wind runs clean and strong. Windows reflect the world above, doubling the sense of space, offering glimpses of something wider than the crowded streets below. On days when the sky is clear and open, people pause on their way to work, looking up—even if just for a moment—to let their minds rise above daily cares. In gardens and small balconies, old watering cans stand ready, gathering droplets left from the morning mist, reminders that each day is a new turn, a chance for something to break free.

Fisherfolk tend their nets beside inland lakes, mending holes while the surface shimmers with reflected light. Their hands know that luck comes and goes, that a bountiful catch depends as much on patience as on the turning of unseen tides. Sometimes, a child will find a feather fallen near the water’s edge, colored as if plucked from the sky itself, a talisman to tuck into a pocket or a diary, marking a day when everything changed.

The wheel spins on. Today, to honor its movement, you might take a walk under the wide, open sky, letting your gaze trace the paths of birds. If you have a chance, wash your favorite pillowcase and hang it where the breeze can find it, or place a wildflower in a glass beside your bed as a sign of openness to change. Keep a small notebook at hand, ready to record the unexpected, and when you sit down at evening, remember that fortune turns for everyone, sometimes imperceptibly, but always in time.
