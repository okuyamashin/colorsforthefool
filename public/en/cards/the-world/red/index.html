Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / THE WORLD

Red

Upright · #FF0000

A wreath of laurel leaves arcs through air, its ends nearly meeting, forming an oval gate through which a lone dancer moves. Arms open, she is framed by four living creatures, each gazing from their corner—lion, bull, eagle, and angel—silent guardians of the four edges of the world. Within her movement, the circuit nears completion; a ribbon waits, unknotted, ready to be tied in celebration and closure. Around her, the world’s journey is mapped—paths crossing mountains, rivers bending, the sky arched above fields and forests.

Long ago, on the edge of civilization, ribbons were tied around trees at the end of a journey. The binding sealed promises and remembered safe returns. In the heart of a city, a market stall displays the fruit more ancient than the road itself: apples, their skins shining with a familiar luster, heaped beside pomegranates split open, each jewel-like seed glimmering with promise. The merchant’s scales tilt as she weighs clusters of cherries, piled high in woven baskets, destined for tables and kitchens. Nearby, a weathered wooden cart is stacked with tomatoes, plucked at dawn, still bearing the scent of earth and vine. These fruits, gathered from gardens and groves, arrive at the same destination—a closing of circuits as hands trade coin for nourishment.

The four living creatures—emblems of the known world—have watched as centuries unfold. In fields, poppies nod in the breeze, their petals recalling battles and the memory of peace hard-won. Along ancient roads, banners unfurl above city gates, high on ramparts and in the hands of marching processions, marking the return of travelers. From kitchens, the steady simmer of a stew draws family and friends to the table, a pot bubbling with peppers and beans, its aroma a signal that the journey of gathering is complete. A kettle whistles, ready to pour over tea leaves, its handle polished by use, a fixture in every household.

At the edge of the Mediterranean, amphorae once held the harvest of vineyards—clusters pressed, their juice transformed into something more enduring, shared among those gathered at feasts. In the far north, autumn brings the work of canning and preserving, hands sliding berries into jars, sealing sweetness for cold months ahead. In tropical forests, birds flash through the foliage, their feathers gleaming as they collect seeds, building nests as the rainy season closes. Each act—tying a ribbon, cooking a meal, preserving the bounty, marking a threshold—speaks of endings made sacred, the last knot tied before a new path opens.

Today, a person might set out a bowl of strawberries on the kitchen counter, scatter a handful of dried cranberries in morning oatmeal, or tuck a silk ribbon into the pages of a book as a marker of completion. An ancient practice endures: tying a string around the wrist for luck, or pinning a flower to a jacket before stepping out into the day, quietly acknowledging the circuit closing and the world’s promise renewed. The living creatures—lion, bull, eagle, angel—watch and remember, bearing witness to the tie that binds beginnings to endings.
