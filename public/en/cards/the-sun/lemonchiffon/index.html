Draw a card / THE SUN

LemonChiffon

太陽 · レモンシフォン

Upright · #FFFACD

The Sun rides high, shedding all confusion, and the garden wakes. In older decks, the child on horseback carries neither sword nor shield, but outstretched arms and trust—knowing the warmth has returned. Behind, the stone wall no longer looms as a boundary, but glows softly, defining shelter from the dark and wind. The illumination of the day is not harsh but embracing, like the hush in a quiet kitchen at morning, the outlines of things restored to gentle clarity.

Across the kitchen table, a slice of sponge cake sits on a plate, dusted with sugar, light as a cloud and threaded with zest. Baked on a spring day, it is the kind of cake that invites sunlight to settle among the crumbs. Once, in Victorian parlors, chiffon cakes were prized for their texture, airy and resilient, made to slice cleanly even in the trembling hands of a first host. The chiffon, a fabric in the same house as the cake, once lined Parisian hats and bridal veils, catching what sun filtered through a window, casting a delicate brightness over the brow and eyes. In both cake and cloth, there is an embrace of lightness—not the absence of weight, but its transformation, an offering.

Elsewhere, in groves on the southern slopes of Sicily, harvesters pluck fruit from old, thorned trees. The fruit is tender-skinned, the pith almost translucent when held up to sun. Markets once bustled with traders carrying baskets lined with straw and silk, each fruit more precious for its journey across the strait. In myth, a single fruit given as a token meant safe passage or new beginnings. The day’s brightness rests inside each rind, waiting to be released into marmalade or cordial, or simply sliced and shared.

Beyond the walls and gardens, there is a certain morning in early June when the air itself dresses in softness. The sky is not blank, but veiled—light diffused through a high, fine cloud cover that brightens the face of a river and the backs of grazing cattle. On such mornings, the world feels newly made, edges gentle, and every surface touched with a calm, filtered radiance. A wind stirs through curtains sewn from old chiffon, the fabric lifting with each current, letting in a breath of hope.

Today, someone might slice a piece of sponge cake and eat it by an open window, letting crumbs fall onto a napkin stitched of the same fabric as the old veils. Another might peel a fruit and leave the rind to curl on the table, its scent filling the room and mingling with the day’s first warmth. It is also a fine moment to open a letter or a favorite book beneath that June sky, tracing the outlines of words returned from shadow. Or to simply let the morning air pass through the lifted curtains, breathing in the world made new.
