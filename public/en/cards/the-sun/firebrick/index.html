Draw a card / THE SUN

FireBrick

Reversed · #B22222

The card’s child rides onward, but the light is endless, stripped of shade. The earth in front stretches dry and cracked, the surface swept by a summer wind that stings the eyes, drawing lines of fatigue along the feet each step. A path, once cool beneath the sun, now radiates the memory of many days’ heat, grit grinding underfoot. The cloth draped over the child’s back, once soft, is stiff from salt and dust, carrying the scent of the vast plain, the feeling of journeys made too long beneath a sky that refuses to dim. In the distance, the low stone wall of an old kiln rises, its square mouth open to the sun, the memory of fires that once raged within still clinging to its hollow core.

There are places where the land itself seems forged for endurance. In villages that cluster near ancient riverbeds, earth is stacked and shaped by hand, every block a testimony to sweat and season. These blocks, burnt hard in fire, settle into homes, wells, and public squares. Underfoot, fragments of failed attempts line the path, chipped edges worn smooth by years of passage. The kiln’s remains mark the edge between what is made and what is left behind: weathered walls, the ground littered with shards, the sun beating down, unstinting, upon both.

Elsewhere, on the long road between towns, travelers learn the lesson of dryness. Shoes are caked with the residue of many miles, soles pressed flat and marked by pebbles. At the hour when shadows vanish, the sensation is not so much of walking as of enduring, a slow negotiation with the land, the promise of distance measured in the tired lift of the foot. The cloth at one’s shoulder is heavy, roughened by use, the color of old embers, holding the memory of campfires burned low at the end of a journey. It is a cloth that has dried many times in the sun, the folds stiff and crackling with dust as it is gathered around the neck.

In the kitchen, a pot simmers on a stove, the sauce within thickening and darkening as it bubbles. The cook leans over, stirring with a wooden spoon, recalling the markets where peppers are heaped in baskets, the scent sharp and earthy. Here, what has been dried by the sun is ground and mixed, turned by slow heat into something that speaks of journeys by caravan, harvests gathered in the hard light, the flavor of far places. The bowl is heavy, fired in a kiln much like the one at the edge of the path, its surface pitted and marked with the memory of flame.

Sometimes, in the heart of dryness, a memory of rain lingers: the press of wet earth turned to brick, the promise that what is parched now might one day be softened. The weather, though, holds steady—an endless, cloudless heat that vibrates in the air, drawing every scent and sound toward stillness. The horizon shimmers, and the only relief is found in the careful work of hands, shaping, tending, preparing for the moment when dusk will finally bring rest.

Today, you might shape bread with your hands, feeling the dough’s resistance and warmth. You could walk a rough path in sturdy shoes, letting each step remind you of long roads and ancient trades. You might steep dried peppers in oil, letting the aroma fill your kitchen, or wrap yourself in a blanket that has known many summers, rough but comforting in its weight.
