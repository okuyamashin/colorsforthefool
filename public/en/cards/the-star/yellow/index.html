Draw a card / THE STAR

Yellow

星 · イエロー

Upright · #FFFF00

The card presents a quiet pool, where water returns to the ground, tracing paths among reeds and stones. Two pitchers, balanced in gentle hands, pour their contents patiently, and above, one great star stands sentinel, circled by smaller lights. This is the moment after a storm, when the ground drinks again, and hope finds roots in earth’s open arms.

In the first gardens beyond the floodplain, a traveler recalled the taste of a small fruit clinging to a bush along the Nile: tart, sweet, and shaped like the dawn, a lantern for the heart. Ancient people pressed these fruits between their palms, the juice catching in bowls beside woven baskets, promising warmth in every sip. As the sun rose higher, fields of ripening wheat bent beneath the breeze, each stalk whispering the same quiet vow: to return nourishment to the land, to feed hands that scatter and gather.

A canary, singing at the edge of a window, heralds morning with a voice that cuts through night’s final hush. The cage is open, for the cage is unnecessary—this bird returns by choice, content to perch on the sill and greet the day. On distant roads, a postman balances letters in a satchel, the canvas catching the first glint of sun as he passes between houses. Each envelope, sealed with care, holds the promise of news, blessings, invitations. In the city, a tram clatters along tracks, its top painted to catch attention and reflect the afternoon’s mercy, protecting travelers from glare and thunder.

Legends from the high mountains tell of the first forge, hammered from stone and clay, where a smith worked beneath a roof of straw. Sparks danced upward as coins and rings took shape, each intended to bring luck or settle debts. In the same region, healers brewed herbal teas with petals that floated and settled, lifting spirits and clearing fever from the mind. A merchant’s ledger, battered but precise, tracked the flow of saffron and silk, noting which ships had docked and which fields awaited harvest. In the kitchen, the cook spread mustard atop bread, a tangy mark for those who tilled, sowed, and reaped.

As the seasons turned, fields of daffodils unfolded on hillsides, petals shaking gently in the wind. High above, a kite soared, its tail twisting in the current, sent skyward by a child’s careful hands. The sky clears after summer thunder, and the world smells of wheat and new beginnings. On temple steps, marigolds are strewn in careful patterns, each bloom a prayer for guests and wanderers alike.

Today, you can slice a lemon into a glass of water and drink it slowly, letting the taste linger. You can set a sprig of forsythia in a jar and place it by the door, a quiet welcome for those arriving home. Wear a silk scarf, smooth and bright, as you step out to greet the morning or deliver a kind word. Stand on your balcony or outside your door after the rain, and breathe the air deeply, remembering wheatfields, open cages, and the steady return of hope.
