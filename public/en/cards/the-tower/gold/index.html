Draw a card / THE TOWER

Gold

Upright · #FFD700

Night hangs over the tower, silent and final. Walls that took lifetimes to build split open in the instant a single streak from the storm finds their weakness. The crown, once secure on the spire, tumbles through crackling air. Windows shatter, stones leap from their patient places. The surge from above speaks in thunder, and what had been sealed within—dust, secrets, and old ambitions—spills out to meet the world.

From deep within the mountain, rivers once carried bright stones to the surface. They were sifted from gravel in freezing streams, pulled from caves by hands numb with cold, carried in leather pouches across wild country. In every age, those bright stones were chased, hidden, melted. A king stored his fortune behind thick iron gates; bodies moved quietly in tunnels beneath the city, tapping for veins that flickered in candlelight. When empires crumbled, some filled their pockets and crossed the desert, risking the sun and bandits to reach new lands with their treasures intact. In silence, a coin might turn in a palm, stamped with a forgotten face; a ring might glint in the bottom of a cup, marking both loss and luck.

When the storm comes, the earth itself cracks open, and the veins hidden below are exposed on the riverbank. Prospectors rush to the water’s edge with pans and dreams, hoping the flakes will stick. At home, a wedding band is slipped from a finger, laid quietly in a dish before tears can fall. In a darkened room, a reliquary is pried open, and a saint’s relic is lifted free, the old lock broken at last. On festival nights, children press their faces to the glass of a shop, longing for the smooth curve of a medal, the flash of a locket, the promise it holds. Farmers toss coins into a new well, asking for rain and harvest. In kitchen drawers, old heirlooms wait, cutlery engraved with initials, once given as a dowry, now tarnished but enduring.

The rush after the lightning is a scramble. Collapse does not give time for sorting; whatever was hidden turns up in the chaos. On battlefields, crowns roll in the mud, picked up by strangers who never wore them. In city alleyways, a tooth-shaped nugget is traded for bread. Under the torchlight of a ruined chapel, someone finds the chalice overturned, spilling its secret cache. The wind carries the cry far—there is something to be claimed, and not all who find it are ready.

What is revealed cannot be hidden again. One may take a coin from a keepsake box and carry it in a pocket while walking, feeling its weight with each step. Melt a broken piece of old jewelry to create something new, marking the transition from what was cherished to what will be worn. Visit a museum and stand before a crown behind glass, letting the story of its journey through history settle in silence. Open a cupboard and polish an old spoon, reminding oneself that even in tumult, what endures can be found and restored.
