DarkRed

塔 · ダークレッド

Reversed · #8B0000

When lightning strikes the highest point, it looks for the swiftest path down, but in the reversed Tower, the bolt hangs in the air, refusing to touch earth. The stonework still stands, but every crack is spread thin with trembling. The wind howls through fractured battlements, yet the banners keep their place, weighted down by rain that never seems to end. It is a night when the sky is a vault and every minute holds its breath. From the ground, the tower seems unbroken, though above, mortar loosens and keeps its secrets.

Below these walls, an ancient orchard grows, planted by hands that are dust now. Old pomegranates, with skins thick as armor and split open along invisible seams, cling to gnarled branches. Their juice stains the stones beneath, sticky and potent, marking the years in slow, stubborn drips. In the kitchen, a copper pot simmers all afternoon, full of the pressed fruits. The air is heavy with their scent; the hearth burns low, as if to coax the sweetness out without letting anything boil over. The cook stirs with a wooden spoon worn smooth, counting out the time as thunder mutters softly beyond the windows.

The roads around the tower run through a land shaped by old battles. The soil remembers. In spring, the vineyards are pruned back to the wood, the vines bleeding sap that dries in dark, secret beads. Wine casks are stacked in cool cellars, carved into the hill, behind doors braced against the shifting earth. In the oldest part of the village, a tanner’s shop works with hides drawn from distant valleys. As leather is cured, the scent deepens, close and almost metallic, and the work goes on behind shuttered windows, day after day, even when storms gather and the streets run with rain.

On certain nights, when the air is thick and close, the sky shivers with the promise of a storm that does not break. People gather in the keep’s hall, cloaks hung near the door, boots set in a row. Someone pours mulled wine into earthen cups, and the flavor is sharp, edged with a memory of summer heat and the iron tang of ancient soil. Old stories are told, voices low, about the years when the tower fell and no one rebuilt it, but tonight it stands, and the doors remain closed. Outside, the last of the daylight slips away, leaving the world pressed close, as if every wall is holding its own silence in place.

Today, you can press the last seeds from a pomegranate, stain your fingertips with juice, and let its memory linger. Let an old copper pot simmer with cloves and fruit, breathing in the scent that binds this moment to those before you. Polish a belt or satchel made by a careful tanner, feeling the weight of years in each stitch. Walk beneath the storm clouds without opening your umbrella, hearing the thunder and trusting the tower to hold a little longer.
