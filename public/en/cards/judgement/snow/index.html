Snow

審判 · スノー

Upright · #FFFAFA

A brilliant trumpet rises above the clouds, its sound a cold gust that rolls across a quiet plain where coffins yawn open. From each, figures emerge, not dragged but called, their faces upturned and expectant. The call is as clear as winter’s first light, the kind that sweeps over iced fields and hushes all the world beneath its silent command. It is not a thunder, but something pale, unyielding, and absolute—like the world after a storm, where nothing is hidden and every track, every breath, is revealed.

In the old stories, when the world was shaped by gods and omens, blizzards were said to be the breath of higher powers. Messengers in northern myths rode through drifts that blanketed the earth, bearing warnings or proclamations. Even the ancient Romans found omens in the frost that settled on their forum stones, reading destiny in the way it clung to statues and rooftops. In the far east, poets wrote of mountains crowned with the quietest shroud, a sign of both endings and awakenings. When the Judgement card rises, it is this silent voice that calls the sleepers, the same whisper that drifts through open shutters in the night and collects on the ledge.

For those who live close to the land, a fresh layer in the morning means more than cold. It is the great equalizer, concealing old scars and tracks, offering a new surface to stride across. Children wake early to see unbroken drifts in the yard, knowing that any mark will be noticed. Farmers measure the weight settled on their barns, sensing what it means for the coming thaw. In cities, rooftops don their thickest hats, and every sound is softened, decisions made in the hush as if the world itself is pausing to listen. Monasteries once kept records of late-season storms, believing each a message, a reminder that nothing buried stays lost forever.

Inside, the same transformation overtakes the household. The arrival is marked by the ritual of shaking out quilts kept in cedar chests, watching steam rise as water for tea meets the cold air near a window. Wood stoves crackle, their warmth a counterpoint to the quiet beyond the glass. Letters are written with mittened hands, promises sealed with a breath that clouds in the still air. Old linens are spread on the table, immaculate and crisp, waiting for gatherings that echo with stories and resolutions. Here, too, Judgement’s call is clear: to rise, to answer, to begin anew beneath a sky that demands honesty and reverence.

Today, you might step out early and be the first to cross an untrodden patch of ground, leaving behind a trail that declares, "I am awake." You could unearth an old journal or forgotten letter, and face what lies inside by bringing it into the open. Brew a pot of clear broth or tea, letting its warmth mark a threshold between what was silent and what is voiced. As dusk falls, draw fresh sheets over your bed, and remember that every calling asks for a response, even if it comes quietly, one breath at a time.
