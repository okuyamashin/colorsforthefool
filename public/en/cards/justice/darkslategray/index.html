Draw a card / JUSTICE

DarkSlateGray

正義 · ダークスレートグレイ

Reversed · #2F4F4F

The card’s figure holds scales in one hand and a sword in the other, but today the seat is empty. The scales hang, one side heavy with stones, the other as light as breath. In the courtroom, benches echo with the memory of arguments, but the judge’s chair sits cool and unused. A sense of waiting pervades: a legal process begun but left unfinished, as if both pen and verdict have slipped away, leaving only the infrastructure of justice to idle in silence.

Elsewhere, the absence of balance is reflected in the landscape after rain. The old quarry, cut deep into the hillside, fills with stormwater under a sky swept by low, restless clouds. Shale fragments line the rim, while below, broken slabs of slate mark the edge of an abandoned path. The air is heavy with the scent of wet earth and stone. On days like this, wind presses through the standing stones, hinting at stories of disputes unresolved, bargains made and then forgotten. Each outcrop, scarred and layered, remembers a time when hands extracted rock for roofing and hearths, trades built on trust and the final word of an overseer who now is gone.

In a damp kitchen, someone stacks plates that have been used but not washed, their edges rough, heavy with yesterday’s meal. On the stove, an iron skillet sits, traces of oil catching the faint light from a clouded window. Above the hearth, a pair of tongs from the forge hangs beside a ledger with pages missing, transaction lines abandoned mid-column. In such a house, routine has faltered. The keeper is elsewhere, and each item is left where its purpose once was, not moved forward, not returned.

The same feeling clings to the narrow streets of a mining town at dusk. The air turns sharp; the cobbles are slick. Shuttered windows keep in the warmth, but outside, a chill settles. Workers’ jackets, sturdy and worn, hang on pegs inside doorways, their pockets weighted with scraps of receipts, bits of slate, and the small tools of a trade that waits for daybreak. Chimneys exhale, but some houses do not send up smoke. In the corners of alleyways, a cart’s wheel lies upturned, its iron rim catching the last bit of daylight, a reminder that without hands to right it, function is lost to neglect.

Across such places, the sky deepens. A weather front draws near, heavy with the promise of rain. The horizon blurs where the hills meet the clouds, and the landscape prepares for a night of stillness. There is no thunder, only a persistent drizzle that soaks the moss growing in the cracks of slate roofs and drips quietly from the eaves. The world holds its breath, awaiting a return, a word, or an action that will tip the scales back toward order.

Today, a person might return a borrowed tool to its rightful place, or set a household ledger in order by finishing a page left incomplete. They might walk to the quarry’s edge to listen to the wind, considering what has been left undone, or polish the iron of a skillet until it shines, restoring purpose to what was idle.
