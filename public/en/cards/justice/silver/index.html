Draw a card / JUSTICE

Silver

Upright · #C0C0C0

On the table of Justice, a blade and a pair of scales lie at rest, their surfaces bright in the noon sun that spills through a high, curtain-draped window. The sword’s edge, straight and sharp, echoes the metal’s promise: what is cut will not be joined until it is made right. The scales measure, arms unwavering, bowls balanced with a precision that neither favors nor dismisses. Between them, the hanging cloth marks the threshold—what is hidden will be revealed, what is tangled will be separated. Noon’s light carves out every detail, allowing nothing to escape its reach.

Throughout the world, this blade’s twin appears in the work of old mines and riverbeds, in the stories of cities that rose on the promise of veins deep underground. In the mountains of Potosí and the hills of Nevada, pickaxes struck hard earth in search of bright seams. Coins pressed from these finds once passed from hand to hand, bearing the faces of rulers, paying for bread, land, and justice alike. In the bustling corners of trading posts, merchants weighed those pieces on their own set of scales, the dull ring of each coin settling debate and confirming truth. The light glances off a polished goblet in a quiet room, returning the watcher’s face unclouded, honest, offering no place for shadows or secrets.

In myth, a swift messenger in winged sandals bore a staff crowned with a pair of coiled serpents, each turn reflecting the promise of negotiation. The fabled apples of the Hesperides, guarded by a sleepless dragon, shone with a surface said to never tarnish, tempting the just and unjust alike. On a storm-lit night, the sky cracks open; a heavy rain starts, then ends, and the world glistens as if washed clean. Drops cling to every branch and railing, catching what little sun returns and laying a brief, shining promise over the ordinary.

In the kitchen, a fork and spoon rest at attention beside a simple plate—each one carrying the marks of years spent in honest service. The curve of the spoon, worn thin by stirring soups, catches the afternoon’s clarity, while the fork tines align, ready to divide and share. In a trunk tucked beneath the stairs, old jewelry waits: a locket shaped like a shield, a ring stamped with a family crest, each object a witness to vows made and kept. Out in the garden, sage leaves flutter in an even wind; their surfaces reflect the sky, and anyone who brushes past leaves with the scent of clean resolve.

To walk in the spirit of Justice today, a person might measure ingredients with care, ensuring fairness in every portion. They might polish a favorite candlestick or utensil, restoring the shine that speaks to diligence and attention. Writing a clear note to right a past confusion or misunderstanding brings cause and effect together, setting the record true. Hanging a sheer curtain allows sunlight to fall across the room, illuminating corners where nothing false can remain.
