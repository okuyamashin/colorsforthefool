Draw a card / JUSTICE

DarkRed

正義 · ダークレッド

Reversed · #8B0000

In the court of Justice, two scales are hung from a beam, each arm held high with the expectation of balance. A feather on one side, a stone on the other. But today, one side sags heavily, the other left waiting. The chair behind the bench sits empty, its carved back facing a sunless window. The ritual of weighing continues, but the heart of the matter is missing, and the room is filled with the hush that follows after a verdict is delayed.

Outside, the wind carries the scent of iron and old earth. There is a memory of a smithy deep in the wood, where a forge once glowed in a low stone cottage. The furnace door yawns open, embers cold, tongs hanging unused on the wall. There, in the past, rough hands drew blades from the coals, their surfaces catching the dancing shadows. In that silence, a pomegranate split on the worktable, seeds scattered across the grain. Each seed is a sealed promise, a debt unpaid, their juice staining fingers and wood alike. In myth, Persephone’s bargain was broken by such seeds, her absence tilting the order of seasons and leaving the world in a pause between growth and withering.

Further west, in an old city, a velvet curtain in a council chamber is drawn, blocking the corridor’s lantern light. Behind it, shelves of legal tomes rest undisturbed. The clerk’s inkpot on the desk is dry, the quill set aside. The wax seal of a letter, stamped hastily with a signet, sits unbroken amid the clutter. The message within waits for eyes that have turned away, for a hand to open it and tip the scales back toward motion.

In the countryside, under heavy skies, the late autumn leaves of a wild hawthorn hedge cling stubbornly to their branches. The first frost has come and gone, but the leaves persist, dark and leathery, hiding their sharp thorns. For centuries, travelers have left offerings beneath these boughs in hopes of fair judgment from unseen spirits. Now, the tokens lie untouched: a coin pressed into the soil, a folded scrap of cloth, a crust of bread gone hard. Without the witness, the pact between the wayfarer and the old hedge is left unresolved.

On the hearth at home, a copper pot simmers unattended, its contents thickening as the fire dies low. The recipe is written in an ancestor’s hand, her words looping slowly across a faded page. Lacking a final pinch of spice, the stew is left unfinished, its aroma filling the kitchen yet promising only half of what was hoped for. At the edge of the table, a pair of pruning shears is left open beside the rind of a pomegranate and the sprig of hawthorn, a reminder of tasks abandoned and the pause in rightful order.

One can strike a match and relight the kitchen fire, stirring the pot to coax out the missing flavor. Seek out the unopened letter, break its seal, and read the words in the dim light of evening. Walk to the hedge and lay your palm gently on the worn bark, acknowledging the pact, even if no one is there to see. Take up the shears, trim back what must be cut, and gather the scattered seeds, returning them to the earth with a silent vow.
