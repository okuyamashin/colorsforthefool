DimGray

正義 · ディムグレイ

Reversed · #696969

The columned hall stands quiet, its benches empty. Where the scales should balance, only a draft stirs, carrying the faint metallic scent from a sword that has not left its scabbard in weeks. Dust gathers unevenly on the marble, settling into neglected corners and tracing the outlines of an absent judge’s shoes. The room, built for clarity and order, finds itself hollowed by silence, as if the very memory of resolution has gone missing. The law books stacked on the desk lean toward each other, spines cracked, pages marked not by verdicts but by time and waiting. The gavel, once the punctuation mark of decision, sits unused, damp with last night’s condensation.

This unreplied call for presence echoes across cities. In old towns built from ancient stone, worn steps lead to administrative doors now barred for repairs no one schedules. Overhead, the sky hangs low, a cloud cover that never quite opens, pressing down on the roofs and the streets with a persistent, featureless drizzle. On days like this, the world seems paused between actions, caught in a limbo where processes begin but do not resolve. The courthouse cat, normally a sly shadow darting between pillars, chooses instead to crouch under a bench, eyes half-shut, waiting for movement that will not come.

Elsewhere, on the plains where the land meets the horizon, people recall stories of vanished judges and the years when no one settled disputes. The fields fallow with last season’s stubble, fences leaning as if hoping for a surveyor who will never arrive. At the crossroads, an old anvil stands—once the center of the farrier’s trade, now left outside to gather the morning’s moisture and a coating of fine grit from the passing wind. Villagers walk past it without looking, their disputes muffled, their promises unkept. The hammer, once raised to shape horseshoes or rivets, hangs on its peg, untouched.

In the kitchen, a kettle sits on a stovetop, neither boiling nor cold, its sides dull and blurred from years of hands and steam. The stovepipe above it, marked with soot, draws the eye upward to the rafters where cobwebs sway with every faint current. Old teapots and baking trays, their surfaces dulled from use and washing, rest on the shelf behind. In the pantry, a sack of lentils split open, its contents trickling out, scattering among the grains of rice and the tiny pebbles from last year’s harvest. The absence of a guiding hand, the one who would sweep, repair, arbitrate, is felt in every corner of the room.

Without the steady rhythm of justice, the world takes on the quiet of a leaden morning. Bridges span the river but are untraveled. Overcoats hang in closets, their linings worn soft from years spent waiting for owners who never come home on time. In the workshop, a carpenter’s plane rests atop a half-shaped table leg, curls of shavings gathered around it like the unspilled secrets of a trial that never reached its conclusion.

Today, you can sweep the hearth and gather the scattered lentils from the pantry floor. You might repair the loose hinge on the kitchen door or polish an old tool in the workshop, letting your hands remember their purpose. If the sky is heavy and unyielding, walk slowly beneath the clouds and listen for the echo of decisions waiting to be made.
