Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / THE LOVERS

FireBrick

Reversed · #B22222

Among the parted trees of the card, two figures stand, a garden’s edge between them. No hand moves to the other; no fruit is offered or taken. The archangel’s voice in the leaves grows still, unheard, as if the air itself thickens with silence. This is the pause before decision, a crossroads not yet crossed, the hush in a kitchen before the argument spills over. In such moments, the walls themselves seem to watch, bearing the heat and stillness of a summer afternoon after rain, when the world holds its breath and the clay roof tiles steam.

They stand in a city where old chimneys rise. Each chimney is built from kiln-fired blocks, stacked with patience, each one made to withstand the decades and the blaze of home. The hands that laid them wore out their gloves, brick after brick, shaping hearths to outlast quarrels and winters. These bricks have seen years when the streets filled with the scent of roasting peppers and lamb, when families pressed together in kitchens, voices rising and falling, never quite touching. Still, the thick walls held the warmth, even as words lost their way.

Above them, in mountain villages half a world away, ovens still glow with heat. Clay domes bake the bread that must split before sharing, the crust breaking open to reveal what lies inside. The bread sits cooling, cradled in a towel on a mantel built from the same fired blocks, the same old craft. There is a memory in these stones: stories of towns rebuilt after the flames, of walls that held when storms hammered down. Yet even the sturdiest materials cannot mend a silence thick as mortar, cannot span the gap when two people turn inward, shoulder to shoulder, without reaching across.

Midday on the street, the paving stones still radiate the warmth from a morning’s sun. Children’s voices echo between the houses, chasing each other past the rows of old kilns where laborers once shaped the earth into living space. Nearby, in an alleyway, a vendor’s cart leans against a wall, and the seller wipes sweat from his brow, waiting for the dull afternoon rush. In weather like this, the world feels baked through. Tempers rise quickly, and words can dry before ever being spoken, dust on the tongue.

In the quiet when choices remain unmade, there is always a way to begin again. A person can knead dough for bread, shaping something with their hands, letting the tension of the day soften in the rhythm. One can sit on a front stoop, bricks warm beneath them, and share a meal or even just a story with a neighbor, bridging the distance if only for a moment. It is possible to clean the old fireplace, clearing away last winter’s ashes in readiness for new warmth, or to walk past the familiar row of chimneys and remember the builders who worked side by side, even when not a word was spoken.
