Violet

恋人 · バイオレット

Upright · #EE82EE

Two people step into a garden where choice itself is the fruit hanging low, within easy reach. It is a place where you hear the buzz of bees and the distant trill of a blackbird, and the air is thick with possibility. The path underfoot is soft, pressed by generations of footsteps, and at its edges, wild pansies and sweet-smelling blossoms grow in humble clumps, inviting touch. They are small, heart-shaped, and delicate, yet enduring—living things that have always flourished between paving stones and along woodland borders, quietly marking the beginning of many stories. This is a garden of decision, not of indecision; here, the act of choosing is what brings two people together, what binds their hands as they walk forward.

Above, the sky stretches wide, but there is an intimacy to this enclosure, as though the world has quietly retreated. In ancient Greece, the same kind of flowers were gathered by girls for festival garlands, woven into crowns for those stepping into partnership or pact. In the alpine meadows of Europe, shepherds would find them in the grass, plucking a single bloom to carry home, a sign of affection too shy for words. In old apothecaries, dried bundles of these blossoms were kept behind glass, believed to soothe the heart and clear the mind—a subtle medicine for those at a crossroads.

Once, a poet in a cold, northern house pressed these petals between pages, believing their scent could outlast the harshest winter. Their perfume drifted in the bedroom air, a reminder of sunlit days, of hands held beneath budding branches. In the kitchens of certain villages, sugared petals found their way onto cakes for weddings: a simple sweetness, promising that whatever is chosen will be cherished. In other times and places, delicate cloths were dyed with these flowers, their fibers taking on a new character, becoming shawls and ribbons for celebrations or quiet rituals.

When spring rains come, brief and gentle, they bring a mist that settles on the petals, making them gleam as though the garden itself is touched by something rare. Lovers might pause under the shelter of an old oak, watching the garden renew itself, knowing that the rain will pass and the earth will yield more blossoms tomorrow. In this moment, choice is not a burden but an opening: the two may pick a flower and press it between the pages of a shared book, or carry it home, or simply leave it where it grows as a sign of respect for beginnings.

Today, you could gather a handful of wild pansies and set them in a small glass near your bedside, a gentle invitation to new connections. You might pause for a moment in the rain, letting it bead on your skin, thinking of all the choices that rest quietly in your own garden. You could bake something sweet, adding candied petals atop as a symbol of intention, or write a letter to someone dear and slip a pressed blossom between the folds.
