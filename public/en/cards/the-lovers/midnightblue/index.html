MidnightBlue

恋人 · ミッドナイトブルー

Reversed · #191970

Two figures stand together beneath the hush of evening, the garden’s old stone wall between them and the world. Their hands hang near each other but never touch, and the air is heavy with things unspoken. Fruit hangs above them, the apple’s skin untouched, tempting but never chosen. The sky stretches overhead in silence, a dome without stars, holding in its breadth the ache of words left unsaid. They are close enough to feel each other’s breath, but the distance between is vast, like the gulf between islands shrouded in mist.

In old stories, fishermen would push their boats out long after sundown, seeking the great catch beneath the silent sky. The sea at this hour is deep and secretive, hiding its bounty and its dangers, and the men row in quiet, keeping their lanterns shuttered, guided only by the line where water meets the night. To cross such a surface is to trust in silence, to take no step forward and yet be carried somewhere all the same. There is a kind of waiting in that journey, a patience that asks for nothing and gives little in return.

Far from the coast, the monastery bells ring for vespers. The soft wool of the monks’ robes gathers the coolness of dusk, and the worn prayer book feels heavy in the hand. It is the hour when even the candles burn low, and the chant is a thread pulled through the thickening quiet. Each brother speaks his part, but the words disappear into the old stones, faded before they reach another ear. The library beyond the cloister holds ledgers and secrets, and the narrow corridor glimmers faintly with the memory of ink, secrets sealed for centuries inside their bindings.

Elsewhere, a single swallow arcs across the evening, vanishing between the rooftops and the shifting edge of night. In the city below, fabric merchants fold lengths of dyed silk onto their tables, rolling them smooth beneath lamps that cast more shadow than light. On the weathered steps, a pot of pressed grape leaves waits to be taken in for supper, untouched, flavor deepening as the evening cools. The sky above holds the weight of distant thunder, a storm moving slowly across the horizon, its arrival uncertain, its promise unfulfilled.

In the quiet of a small room, a letter rests on the desk, each line careful, the pen set aside before the final sentence. The window is open to the night air, and the curtain moves with the same hush that fills the spaces between spoken words. On the shelf, a battered detective novel lies unopened, its story paused at a scene where the answer hovers just out of reach. On the stand, a ceramic mug sits cooling, steam no longer rising, waiting for the drinker to return.

You can walk beneath the last glow of dusk, letting silence settle without needing answers. If you have a letter unsent, tuck it beneath the weight of an old book, leaving it for another night. You might steep a cup of tea and drink it slowly near the window, letting the air carry away the thought of unfinished conversations. Or simply fold a soft blanket at the foot of the bed as you listen for the first touch of rain on the roof.
