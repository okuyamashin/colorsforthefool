Draw a card / DEATH

Snow

死神 · スノー

Upright · #FFFAFA

On the card, the rider approaches with a blossom held gently aloft, petals pale and unmarked, its shape an emblem of peace after the end. The horse moves through a world paused between what has finished and what will come, hooves soundless on a ground hidden beneath a silent covering. In the distance, two towers frame the arrival of morning, the sky’s edge glowing softly, revealing a new outline born from the close of night.

That flower, so quiet in the rider’s grip, is kin to those that bloom just as winter loosens its hold. In quiet landscapes where fields rest under a silent sheet, a person opens the door at dawn and steps onto the soft surface, leaving an impression where none was before. The countryside hushes; rooftops, branches, and forgotten carts wear a cold, untouched layer. In the early hours, everything is muffled—each breath a lingering cloud, each footstep a gentle press against what has landed. Across mountain valleys and open steppe, the world seems cleansed, every roof and field blank, old scars covered, patterns left only by the passage of wind or the tracks of an animal.

There are tales from the high north and deep forests, where rivers hide beneath thick crusts and travelers read their safety in the subtle shifts and the way light glances off the surface. In some places, children shape the ground into figures or slide along roads, their gloves and scarves gathering the fine granules that fall through the air. In cities, the landscape changes overnight: steps and benches, cars and ledges become new, as if someone pressed a veil over the old world while all were sleeping. Even the busiest streets grow silent, wrapped in a stillness that invites reflection.

Indoors, families reach for thick duvets and covers, folding them with care at the change of season. The stove gives off a steady warmth, and windows are edged with frost so delicate it looks painted. In kitchens, rice cakes or steamed buns, dusted with powdered sweetness, are brought out for breakfast, recalling the traditions of distant villages where such treats mark the time of beginnings. Fables speak of cranes striding across open fields, their prints the only marks in an untouched expanse, moving forward while all else holds its breath.

The end, as brought by the rider, is an invitation to pause and let the silence settle. What is finished is gently covered, the land prepared for the next outline to emerge. Any harsh edge or sign of struggle fades for a while beneath the soft fall, allowing new shapes to be imagined, new stories to begin. The world waits, restful and expectant.

This morning, you might trace your finger along the surface of a camellia petal, open a window and feel the cold air, or press fresh linen smooth across the bed. Pour warm tea and notice the quiet, allowing yourself to mark this day as a turning point. Pause in the hush after a storm, before busy hours return, and consider what is quietly ending—and what might soon unfold.
