Draw a card / DEATH

Brown

死神 · ブラウン

Reversed · #A52A2A

The card’s rider, who should be passing, lingers on the field, gripping a lopsided crown forgotten by time. The ground is hard beneath slow hooves, churned by the weight of things not yet relinquished. Stalks of wheat fallen last harvest remain unturned, their roots clinging to earth, resisting the plow’s new furrow. The sun stands low, its promise of renewal unnoticed. Instead, the air is heavy with the scent of peat, where old roots and leaves refuse to surrender to rot, keeping yesterday’s shape in the soil.

An ancient fur cap, inherited from generations, sits upon a hook by the hearth. It was made to collect the warmth of bodies now gone, and still, it is worn on frosted mornings, the lining patched again and again. In a low-ceilinged kitchen, a wooden breadbox holds the last crust from a loaf baked days ago. The heel is hard, but no one throws it away; someone will soak it in broth tonight, making it into something nearly new. Along the windowsill, chestnuts from last autumn wait, too hard to eat now, but kept for their memory of a season not quite let go.

Beyond the window, fields stretch to a line of bare oaks, their bark thick and furrowed as though every winter has been etched into them. The ground is damp where the river flooded weeks before, leaving behind a crust of silt. The riverbank’s reeds are matted with last year’s growth, never quite washed away. In the shed, leather harnesses hang from a peg, worn smooth by decades of use; they are polished every spring, even though the horse is long gone, as if the act of care itself can hold time still.

In one corner of the house, a carved bowl sits on a shelf, shaped from the heartwood of a walnut tree felled years earlier. The bowl’s surface carries scratches from spoons, faint rings from cider trickled at gatherings past. At the bottom of the bowl, acorns meant for a child’s game are left untouched. On the table, a clay mug—its handle chipped—remembers when it was part of a set, but the others have vanished, broken or given away. Yet, this one endures, filled each morning with the strongest brew.

At dusk, the wind shifts, bearing the smell of tilled fields, smoke from distant chimneys, and the promise of rain. Change whispers at the threshold, but inside, lamps are trimmed, shoes are cleaned and set beside the door, and hands reach for the oldest walking stick leaning in the corner. A person here might begin by mending a basket whose weave has loosened, or by sweeping the steps that carried so many before. They could plant a walnut or chestnut beside the barn, letting roots dig deep into sleep-heavy ground. Or, sitting by the stove, they might shape new handles from fallen branches, fitting them to well-worn tools, so the work can go on.
