Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / DEATH

Black

Upright · #000000

A rider moves forward, carrying a pale bloom, while dawn threads its first light between two distant towers. The field beneath the horse is still, marked by what has been left behind. Above, the sky deepens, holding the hush of all that has finished. The towers stand as silent witnesses, their shapes unchanged but their purpose shifting as the day breaks. In this space, one chapter completes itself, leaving room for the lines that have yet to be written.

In old stories, the raven is a companion to such moments—neither wholly feared nor entirely welcome. It claims the top of a barren tree or the roof beam of an abandoned chapel, pausing as if to observe the changing world. Along ancient roads, lumps of jet are found, stones formed from ancient wood pressed by centuries, gathered by travelers as tokens to mark the closure of one journey and the start of another. On the hearth, the remains of last night’s fire rest in a bed of residue, smoldering reminders that warmth and light are always borrowed, never kept.

In the quiet corners of daily life, objects hold memory and patience. The cast-iron cauldron sits over the embers, reliable for generations, its surface marked with the story of countless meals. From it, soup is ladled, simple and sustaining, brewed from what remains after the harvest has been stored or eaten. Folding screens made from lacquered wood divide rooms at midnight, offering privacy and permission to leave one side behind and cross into another. In the study, a fountain pen filled with deep ink stands ready—each line traced on thick paper carries the certainty of endings and the openness of beginnings.

Across the landscape, night offers a pause. In the high mountain passes, nomads shape their tents from tightly woven wool, staking them against wind and cold. Inside, the world is quiet, and outside, the darkness holds the promise of another path. In monasteries, before the first bell, monks grind ink on stone, preparing to write prayers, each stroke a record of what must be relinquished and what may be hoped for. The ink does not forget; it memorializes both closure and intent.

Weather, too, plays its part: a thundercloud gathers above the fields, its arrival signaling a break in routine. Rain falls steadily, drumming on slate rooftops, carrying away debris and clearing the air. The streets shine, rinsed of yesterday’s dust, reflecting the new day’s faint glow. In the hush after the rain, the city feels emptied, waiting for footsteps that will draw the next pattern.

If you wish to mark the moment, you might sip tea brewed in a metal pot, letting the steam wind through the cool air as you consider what is ready to be set aside. You could sweep the last of last season’s ashes from the fireplace and scatter them among the roots of a perennial. Take a notebook bound in dark leather, open to a fresh page, and write what you are ready to release. Or, step outside in the hour before sunrise and stand beneath the oldest tree, listening for the rustle of wings—reminders that every end prepares the ground for something not yet imagined.
