Draw a card / THE EMPEROR

Gray

皇帝 · グレイ

Upright · #808080

A throne made from quarried rock stands in a high chamber, its edges chiseled, its face pitted with the slow memory of rain. Behind this seat, the land rises in crags and ridges, where wind and time have ground the bones of mountains into dust. The world is not chaotic here; there are lines, boundaries, and the certainty of land’s division. The Emperor’s place is to sit firmly, to hold the edge where wildness ends and the work of hands begins.

In old tales, the city’s walls were built from stone carried on the backs of oxen, each block squared and shaped by tools that spark when struck. The masons wore aprons of tightly woven linen, smudged from the dust of their labor, and their hammers rang in the morning air. Roads were laid out along the ancient riverbeds, each step guided by surveyor’s rods and marked with pebbles collected from distant hills. The Emperor’s order was not a shout, but the steady instruction of rules—each stone must fit, each gate must close, each law laid as evenly as a mason’s line.

In the north, fishermen turned to the sky before casting nets, reading not the sun’s glare but the curtain above, dense with the promise of rain. They knew the scent of coming storms, the hush that spread as clouds pressed low, and the soft clatter of slate tiles as the wind picked up. Shepherds, too, watched the rise of mist across pasture, the way it wrapped the flock and softened the world’s sharpness. On days when the air became dense and cool, the city’s watchmen patrolled the ramparts wrapped in woolen cloaks, the fabric thick and unshowy, warming without drawing the gaze.

In kitchens, the stone of mortars and pestles held the memory of use—the smoothness worn by seeds and salt ground over generations. Ovens were built from the same material as walls, their bellies lined with ash and their mouths ringed by bread rising in the early dawn. Here, order was warmth and the daily provision of food. Spoons hewn from pewter waited beside steaming bowls of barley soup. Housewives washed the family’s garments on river stones, letting them dry in air heavy with the scent of rain, knowing the work was never finished but always begun again.

The Emperor’s strength is not in sword or decree, but in ritual and procedure, in the knowledge passed from hand to hand. Mountain passes mark the edge of one land and the beginning of another. Boundaries are mapped in the ledger, in the stone wall built to mark the last field, in the ring of cobbles around the wellhead. Even the sky bends to this order, its overcast mood offering a quieter light for work and watchfulness.

Today, walk outside and feel the firmness of the ground beneath your feet, tracing the lines that mark your garden or path. Find a smooth pebble and keep it in your pocket as a steadying weight. Knead bread or grind spices in a stone bowl, taking part in a work that endures. Wrap yourself in the plain comfort of a woolen shawl, and listen to the hush of the world before rain.
