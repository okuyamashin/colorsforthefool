Draw a card / THE EMPEROR

Snow

Reversed · #FFFAFA

The Emperor’s throne stands silent, carved arms and empty seat catching the morning chill. The hollowed-out power invites neither ruler nor supplicant; the pillars once holding the ceiling feel unanchored, stern but senseless. The banners hang rigid, their creases sharper than command. The old world’s order lingers, a skeletal frame where process is king and heart has fled.

Outside, winter presses against the walls. The landscape stretches, blanketed and unmoved, concealing the ground’s memory with drifts sculpted by wind alone. There is no voice, only the measured hush of falling weather, each flake a detail repeating itself in endless patterns. The roofs bear weight that will not melt soon, their ridges and slates transformed by accumulation, burdened for the season. The paths between houses are marked not by footsteps but by what is left untouched, a memory of movement erased as soon as it takes place.

In such a season, the fields sleep beneath their cover. Where armies once marched or councils once assembled, all is now mute and pinned beneath the sky’s cold hand. Trees stand upright, their branches outlined in rime, the architecture of order without any leaves or birds to fill its purpose. On the highest slopes, the wind chisels the crust into unfamiliar shapes, so that the land itself seems ruled by an absent mind, a court of silence. No decree is given; the discipline of weather is its own authority, impartial and stern, insistent on uniformity. Even the river, swift in other months, has slowed beneath its sheath, clinging to habit more than flow.

Inside, tasks continue because they must. The hearth is swept with brisk strokes, gathering the day’s fine powder before it can melt and soak the floor. The door is barred at twilight, keeping out the cold’s advance; the ritual is older than memory, obeyed without question. Bread is baked, rising in the oven while the world outside slumbers, a gesture toward sustenance rather than celebration. The linens, aired and shaken, are laid with precision, crisp folds mirroring the order of the season. In the larder, roots and preserved fruits are inventoried, each row an echo of last year’s harvest, prepared with the diligence of those who expect nothing unexpected.

History, too, holds its chill. There are tales of emperors who built palaces in high latitudes, their halls glazed with patterned frost, their courts subdued beneath the glittering ceilings. Travelers in those realms recall the way silence gathers at the edges, how every footstep vanishes behind them, and how the light finds no purchase on the world but the faint glimmer on ledges and rails. In these stories, the empire is less a gathering of people than a discipline imposed on the land, an ideal of structure surviving in the absence of intent.

Today, one might set a bowl outside to catch what falls, then bring it in to watch it dissolve. One could brush the threshold clean before sunrise, making a ritual of readiness, or linger a moment at the window to count the slow drift and remember how stillness can rule a world. Or, in the quiet of the evening, write a letter structured with care, each line a measured act, letting the order of words stand in for presence that is temporarily gone.
