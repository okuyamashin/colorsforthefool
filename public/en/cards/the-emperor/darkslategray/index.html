Draw a card / THE EMPEROR

DarkSlateGray

Reversed · #2F4F4F

A throne stands on a windswept mountain, its foundation carved from ancient stone. Once, footsteps echoed in command, but now moss creeps over the chiseled arms and the seat is empty. The scepter, once held tight, lies abandoned on the flagstones, its iron head dulled by storms. The banners that once fluttered above have become frayed, hanging limp in the drowsy air. Here, the impression of order is preserved through arrangement, but the heart that once beat beneath crown and mail has withdrawn. Rules abide, etched in stone and oath, but meaning has seeped away, leaving only the scaffolding of authority.

Not far from the throne, a slate quarry scars the hillside. Men once chipped and hauled, the rhythm of their hammers ringing out like the enforcement of law. Now, only the memory of labor remains. Rain collects in the cuts and grooves, forming silent pools. Mule carts, their axles rusted and wheels half-buried, rest in the shade of slate walls. A crow perches atop a forgotten worker’s cap, peering into the pit as though searching for the hand that once wore it. The quarry’s edges fade into a copse of yew trees, their twisted trunks clutching the boundary between discipline and decay.

In the lowlands, a smithy stands locked. The forge is cold; the anvil, once an instrument of mastery, now absorbs the stillness of absence. A single blade—its edge neither sharpened nor dulled—rests across a wooden workbench. Beside it, a ledger records meticulous measurements and lists of tools, each item ticked with care. Dust has settled on the pages, softening the lines but not erasing the record. The discipline of process endures, even when the craftsman does not return.

Storm clouds gather over distant ridges, muting the light. The air holds the taste of approaching rain, metallic and cool. The wind presses against the land, tracing the outlines of bare rock and empty outbuildings. In old cities, rooftops of overlapping stone press tight to one another, warding off the drizzle. Alleyways glisten, their puddles reflecting empty windowsills where, in another season, pots of rosemary might have stood. Bells toll in the church tower, their sound carried low and heavy through the damp, reminding the listener of a structure maintained mostly by promise and habit.

At the hearth, a cast-iron kettle sits atop a trivet, and a long-handled poker rests against the bricks. These objects remain in their places, awaiting the return of hands to stir, to tend, to kindle warmth. In the cupboard, a mortar carved from riverstone—its surface worn smooth by generations—waits for seeds or salt to meet its intent. Each tool, each vessel, holds a memory of use, but now they serve as proof that function can outlast the touch that gave it purpose.

To inhabit this space today, one might walk on the moor after rainfall, treading paths set by quarrymen, boots scraping loose shards beneath the gorse. You might sit beside a cold hearth and set a kettle to its stand, listening to the hush where fire once crackled. You could turn the pages of a ledger or calendar, tracing lists made by another, and mark your own line beneath. Or choose to sit quietly near a window as storm clouds drift in, letting the world’s persistence be enough.
