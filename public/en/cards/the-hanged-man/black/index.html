Today's lucky color, drawn from the tarot

Draw a card / THE HANGED MAN

Black

Reversed · #000000

When the Hanged Man appears upside down, the world seems to pull at his ankles, demanding motion when none is possible. He hangs above a riverbank, suspended from the gnarled roots of a tree, the current below rushing on though he remains captive. His world is not one of surrender, but of refusal—the body wants to flee, yet the branches hold firm, and the river beneath refuses to pause its journey to the sea. In this suspended time, the mind flails, unwilling to rest in its new position, struggling against the hush that comes before real movement. In the distance, a storm gathers, the air close and heavy, promising a sky with no stars.

Such weather presses upon the fields and lanes. Thunderheads stack against the horizon, blotting out distant hills. The clouds build themselves into walls, hard and unyielding, and the wind dies before their arrival. In this waiting, farmers shut their doors and windows, and travelers search for shelter beneath low stone bridges. Standing at the threshold, a person watches the world fade. Trees become silhouettes, and the familiar path unspools into nothingness—a world erased by the approach of the storm’s veil.

History records nights when the city’s lamps failed and the streets filled with silence, interrupted only by the clatter of hurried feet. People shut their shutters and drew thick curtains, closing themselves into rooms that swallowed the last traces of dusk. There, the tools of work and rest gather: a cast-iron kettle, mottled with years of use, warming itself over the embers; a stone mortar, heavy in the palm, pressing herbs into powder; a strip of velvet thrown over the back of a kitchen chair, waiting for hands to smooth it into shape. In the cupboards, licorice root and the unyielding hulls of roasted seeds remind the tongue of stubbornness and persistence.

Beyond these walls, volcanic fields lie fallow under the weight of cooling ash. Here, seeds sleep for seasons, locked in the earth’s embrace, awaiting the moment when rain and sun find them. Travelers who pass through these lands speak of obsidian shards underfoot—sharp fragments from ages past, reminders that creation often follows destruction. In the morning, crows gather where the earth is bare, their wings cutting arcs above the stilled landscape. They are drawn to what is hidden, to what waits beneath the surface for its chance to rise.

There are moments in myth when heroes must descend—into caves, into the Underworld, into the uncertain hush after defeat. Their journeys demand patience and a willingness to listen. Yet in this card’s reversed state, the lesson is refused: the descent becomes a prison, the silence a torment. It is an exile that grows heavier with each attempt to break free. The world offers no path forward, only the challenge of waiting, and the fear that comes with not knowing what the waiting will yield.

Today, you can close the shutters before the storm arrives, keeping the gathering hush outside for just a while. You might run your palm across the surface of the old kettle, feeling the heat that lingers long after the fire has burned out. Consider grinding roots and seeds by hand, letting the steady weight teach your thoughts the patience that the world demands. Or simply sit quietly, listening for distant thunder, and allow yourself to rest in the knowledge that some waits are long, but not without purpose.
